LOCAL EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS FOR THE 3D COMPRESSIBLE
NAVIER-STOKES/CAHN-HILLIARD SYSTEM WITH VACUUM
YINGHUA LI, JINGXUE YIN, AND HAORAN ZHENG ∗
Abstract.We establish local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions to the compress-
ible Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system in a bounded smooth domain inR 3, allowing the initial
density to vanish. The initial data satisfy suitable regularity and compatibility conditions for the
momentum and phase equations. The main difficulty arises from the simultaneous degeneracy
of the momentum equation and the density-weighted phase equations, which complicates the
construction of compatible positive-density approximations and uniform initial estimates. We
overcome this difficulty by introducing a time-independent residual into the chemical relation.
The residual preserves the phase compatibility exactly, keeps the initial phase field unchanged,
and vanishes inH 1 as the approximation parameter tends to zero. For each positive-density
approximation, we construct a local strong solution using a semi-discrete Galerkin scheme that
discretizes only the phase variables and retains the full transport and momentum equations. We
then derive a priori estimates independent of the density lower bound, which yield a common
lifespan and allow us to pass to the vacuum limit. Uniqueness is proved using a modified differ-
ence energy that accounts for the coupling between the density and the chemical potential. To
our knowledge, this is the first local well-posedness result for the three-dimensional compressible
Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system with initial vacuum.

The NSCH system is a diffuse interface model for two-phase flows that allows topological
changes of the interface. In the classical sharp interface formulation, the interface between two
immiscible fluids is represented by a moving hypersurface; the fluid equations are imposed on
the two sides of the interface and are coupled through suitable jump conditions. Although this
description is physically natural, it becomes difficult to handle when interfaces merge, split, touch
the boundary, or undergo large deformations. The phase-field approach avoids this geometric
obstruction by replacing the sharp interface with a thin transition layer and introducing an
order parameterϕto distinguish the two phases. The Cahn-Hilliard equation then describes
conservative phase separation driven by a chemical potential, while the Navier-Stokes equations
govern the bulk fluid motion.
This viewpoint can be traced back to the dynamic classification of Hohenberg and Halperin
[22], where the first Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard model, also known as Model H, was introduced.
The system was later derived by Gurtin, Polignone and Vi˜ nals [21] within the framework of
continuum mechanics, and by Liu and Shen [27] through an energetic variational approach.
Model H describes incompressible binary mixtures with constant total density, corresponding
to the homogeneous case. We refer to [2, 7, 8, 17–19] for well-posedness results on it. A natural
generalization of Model H is given by nonhomogeneous incompressible mixtures, where the
mixture density is treated as an independent unknown and the mass-averaged velocity is assumed
to be divergence-free; see [15, 20, 33]. For unmatched-density and quasi-incompressible models,
we refer to [1,3,5,32].
Compared with the incompressible theory, rigorous results for compressible NSCH systems
remain far more limited. Compressible and quasi-incompressible diffuse interface models were
developed by Lowengrub and Truskinovsky [28]. A mathematically tractable compressible dif-
fuse interface model was later proposed by Abels and Feireisl [4]. Kotschote and Zacher [23]
established local-in-time existence and uniqueness of strong solutions for the compressible NSCH
model derived in [28]. More recently, Elbar and Poulain [14] studied a generalized compress-
ible NSCH model with friction effects and proved the existence of global weak solutions under
simplifying assumptions. Other related topics, including stationary problems [24, 25], moving
contact lines [18], dynamic boundary conditions [9], and low Mach number limits [6], have also
been investigated for NSCH systems.
When vacuum is taken into account in compressible two-phase flows, vanishing-density states
arise as a limiting idealization of extremely low-density regions, although vapor typically retains
a nonzero density. Such regions are physically relevant, for example, in liquid-vapor flows in-
volving cavitation or strong expansion, or during rupture events, where an interfacial region is
evacuated as a liquid filament pinches off. Recently, Ding, Li, Yan and Zheng [13] established
global existence and uniqueness of strong solutions allowing vacuum in the one-dimensional
setting. The corresponding problem in the multidimensional case, raised in [20] for the incom-
pressible nonhomogeneous model and in [13] for the compressible one, remains open.

To the best of our knowledge, this paper establishes the first local well-posedness result for
strong solutions to the three-dimensional compressible NSCH system (1.2)–(1.4) with initial
vacuum. The main difficulty comes from the presence of vacuum. Whenρis allowed to vanish,
the momentum equation becomes degenerate, and one cannot divide by the density to recoverut
orϕ t. For compressible Navier-Stokes equations with vacuum, suitable compatibility conditions
are usually imposed to ensure that the initial material acceleration is square-integrable. This
approach goes back to Salvi and Straˇ skraba [30] and was further developed in [10–12]. In the
NSCH system, however, the Cahn-Hilliard coupling introduces an additional degeneracy: the
chemical relation
ρµ=−∆ϕ+ρΨ ′(ϕ)
cannot be solved forµby division byρon the vacuum set, while the phase equation
ρ(ϕt +u· ∇ϕ) = ∆µ
does not directly control the unweighted phase derivativeϕ t. On the set whereρvanishes,
the momentum equation loses its inertial and pressure terms, the chemical relation reduces to
∆ϕ= 0, and the phase equation reduces to ∆µ= 0: the phase field and the chemical potential
are harmonic on the vacuum set, the capillary stress vanishes, and no evolution law forϕis
available. Consequently, direct regularization of the density does not automatically preserve the
phase compatibility needed for uniform initial estimates. We resolve this difficulty by preserving
the initial phase field and absorbing the incompatibility of the chemical relation into a time-
independent residual, which vanishes inH 1 as the approximation parameter tends to zero.
While the diffuse interface formulation permits topological changes at the level of the model,
analyzing them rigorously, such as whether a thinning filament ruptures in finite time, requires
the pointwise information provided by strong solutions. Since topological changes are precisely
where vacuum regions form, a local strong theory with vacuum is a prerequisite for such ques-
tions. Our main result is stated as follows.

to preserve the initial compatibility conditions needed for uniform estimates.
