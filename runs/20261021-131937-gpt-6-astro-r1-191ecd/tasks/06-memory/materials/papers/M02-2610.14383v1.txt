UNIQUENESS AND STABILITY OF ENTROPY WAVES AMONG VANISHING
DISSIPATION LIMITS FROM THE NAVIER–STOKES–FOURIER SYSTEM
COOPER FAILE, INGRID LACROIX-VIOLET, AND ALEXIS F. VASSEUR
Abstract. In this paper, we establish the stability of general entropy wave solutions of the
compressible Euler equations (that is, profiles with constant velocity and pressure) in the class
of vanishing dissipation limits from the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system. If the initial data of our
sequence of solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system converges to the initial data of the
entropy wave solution, we further have convergence of the solutions to the entropy wave. This gives
uniqueness and stability of the entropy wave in this class. The proof is based on the a-contraction
theory.

There have been several works on the uniqueness of entropy waves and related solutions to the
Euler equations (1.1). The case of planar contact discontinuities without shear among vanishing
dissipation limits from a Navier–Stokes–Fourier system via the method of a-contraction was first
established by the third author and Wang in the 1-d case [VW15] and later Kang, the third author,
and Wang proved in the 3-d case [KVW21]. We note that the existence of weak solutions to the
Navier–Stokes–Fourier system for the constitutive relations considered in [KVW21] remains open.
Wang, Zhang, and Zhao proved the nonlinear stability of entropy waves under the Taylor sign
condition. Specifically, they describe the evolution of the boundary of an interface of discontinuity,
and show stability provided the Taylor sign condition holds [WZZ24]. In this work, we study waves
with pM ≡const , so we are outside the scope of [WZZ24]. We note that due to the constant velocity
assumption, our result does not apply to vortex sheets or contact discontinuities with shear. These
structures are well known to exhibit Kelvin–Helmholtz instability and can admit infinitely many
weak solutions. For such examples, see [KS25] where the authors study 2-d compressible Euler
with a particular pressure and [MS23] where the authors show non-uniqueness of vortex sheets
for 2-d incompressible Euler. In the setting of smooth solutions, Feireisl proved stability under
vanishing dissipation limits from the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system, with a specific relation between
the radiative coefficientaand dissipation coefficientsνandκ[Fei16].
