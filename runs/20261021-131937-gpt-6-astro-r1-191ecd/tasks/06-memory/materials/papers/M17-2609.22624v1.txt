Abstract.We provide explicit bounds on the number of zeros forζin short
intervals near the edge of the critical strip. Ultimately we show that even in
short intervals a positive proportion of zeros will not be near the edge of the
critical strip. For example withh >100andt >10 100 the proportion of zeros
ofζwith imaginary part in[t−h, t+h]which have real part in[ 1
16 , 15
16 ]is at
least13%and a positive proportion is already obtained att >10 43.
The aim of this article is to provide bounds on the number of zeros in small
sections of the critical strip, particularly along the boundary of the critical strip.
We will look at primarily two types of regions, circular, and rectangular.
Throughout the article we shall denote by
•N(T)the number of zeros,ρofζwith imaginary0<ℑ(ρ)≤T.
•N(s, α)the number of zerosρofζwith|ρ−s| ≤α.
•N(T 1, T2, α)the number of zerosρofζwithT 1 ≤ ℑ(ρ)≤T 2 andℜ(ρ)>
1−α.
In all cases zeros are counted with multiplicity and we shall maintain the convention
that zeros on the boundary count with1/2their multiplicity. We note that using
this last convention, as opposed to including or excluding the boundary does not
impact the bounds we would prove except for the degenerate casesT= 0,α= 0,
orT 1 =T 2.
Note that other sources writeN(σ, T)for the number of zerosρofζwith
0<ℑ(ρ)≤Tandℜ(ρ)> σ. We don’t anticipate this causing any confusion.
There are many results on zero density, however most of these considerT2 −T 1 to
be large in comparison to the size ofT2. Our aim is to treat much smaller intervals.
At the same time, we note that bounds on the number of zeros in short intervals are
a key ingredient to a common strategy for proving bounds on zeros in long intervals
(see for example [5]).

minimal values fort0 for which a positive proportion result is attainable.
This result is in some ways qualitatively weaker than other estimates about
zeros being on or near the critical line. For example the approach of Levinson, as
improved by Conrey and then Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu and Zeindler (See [14,26,
32]) establishes that, ast→ ∞, a little over 40% are on the critical line. However,
the approach only appears to work on long intervals, that is, intervals of length at
leastω(T). In fact, since roughly40%of the zeros less than(5/4)Tare less than
(3/4)Tthe result barely tells you that in an interval of widthT /2at heightTthere
must be any zeros on the critical line. The recent claimed improvement to2/3in
[1] doesn’t fundamentally alter this. Karatsuba’s proof of Selberg’s conjecture does
lower that threshold to powers ofT, and subsequent work ultimately establishes
that almost every relatively short (sub-exponential inlog(T)) interval contains zeros
on the critical line (see [24]). But almost all intervals still isn’t all, and they still
aren’t obtaining a positive proportion. Standard zero-density results going back at
least to Bohr and Landau (see [8]) tell us 100% of zeros are withinϵof the critical
line. But again this tells us nothing about short intervals. In the opposite direction,
results of Montgomery in fact tell us that if there are zeros very near the edge of
the critical strip, there will somehow be many of them (see [27, Chapter 11]).

One approach we use relies on bounds forN(s, α). As such we provide updated
explicit versions of such bounds in Theorems 16 and 18. We note that such bounds
onN(s, α)are used in many applications directly. For example in log-free zero-
density results (see for example [5, Corollary 2.3] and [29, Lemmas 1 and 2]).
Various approaches to zero-free regions use these types of results as well (see for
example [4,17,27]). Bounds like those in Theorems 2 and 3 also have the potential
to be used to improve other zero density results such as in [3,5,30] where there can
be benefit in bounding the number of zeros in a rectangular region rather than a
square one.
We organize this paper as follows:
•In Section 1 we apply Jensen’s theorem to boundN(s, α). In order to
provide sharp bounds we provide methods to improve estimates for the
integral inside the critical strip in Section 1.1 and outside the critical strip
in Section 1.2.
•In Sections 2 and 3 we provide bounds onN(t−h, t+h, α)arising from
two different zero detectors, the first of which is better forαsmaller, and
the second better forαlarger.
•In ordertoimplementthe approachof Section3 inSection 3.1weprovide an
improved mechanism to bound the arg integrals which arising as secondary
error terms in more standard zero density results.
•In Appendix A we provide a number of explicit bounds forlog|ζ(s)|and
log(Γ(s))which we needed elsewhere.
There are a number of avenues of future exploration which could improve the
resulthere, particularlyforsmallt. Firstly, therearemanychoicesforzerodetectors
we could use forN(s, α). In this article we currently use a classical version of
Jensen’s formula. It would be interesting to explore other detectors such as those
in [5,17,18,20,35]. As these methods tend to involve estimating| ζ′

A second natural direction of exploration is parts of our analysis away from the
