Abstract.Recent analyses combining DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements with
other cosmological observations have reported indications of departures from the ΛCDM model within
dynamical dark energy scenarios. We investigate whether such deviations are reproduced consistently
inf(T) gravity and whether they persist when different cosmological observations are combined.
Using the DESI DR2 BAO measurements, Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), and Pantheon+
Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) sample, we constrain three representativef(T) models: the power-law,
exponential, and hyperbolic-tangent models. We compare the constraints obtained from different
combinations of data, distinguishing changes in marginalized parameter distributions from changes
in the relative statistical performance of the models. For the power-law model, the mild deviation
from the ΛCDM model obtained from BAO+CMB disappears after the SNe Ia data are included. In
contrast, the exponential model retains a preference for a nonzero deformation parameter in the full
analysis, while the allowed parameter space of the hyperbolic-tangent model changes substantially
after the CMB distance priors are included. Importantly, the parameter-level deviation found for the
exponential model does not lead to a clear statistical preference over the ΛCDM model according to the
information criteria, whereas the hyperbolic-tangent model shows a poorer statistical performance for
the combined data. Despite these differences in the model constraints, the inferred transition redshift
zT remains relatively stable among the three models and data combinations. Our results show that
the departures from the ΛCDM model indicated by DESI analyses are not a generic feature off(T)
gravity, but depend strongly on the specific modified gravity parameterization and on the combination
of cosmological observations.
Keywords:modified gravity, baryon acoustic oscillations, dark energy.

1 Introduction
Over the past few decades, a variety of cosmological observations have established that the Universe
is undergoing a phase of accelerated expansion [1, 2]. In the standard ΛCDM model, this acceler-
ation is driven by a cosmological constant, which provides the simplest description of dark energy
(DE). Although ΛCDM has been remarkably successful in explaining a wide range of cosmological
and astrophysical observations, it still faces both theoretical and observational challenges. On the
theoretical side, the extremely small observed value of the cosmological constant compared with the-
oretical expectations gives rise to the well-known fine-tuning and cosmic coincidence problems [3, 4].
On the observational side, discrepancies have emerged between cosmological parameters inferred from
early- and late-Universe probes [5–12]. If these discrepancies cannot be fully attributed to systematic
effects, they may point to physics beyond the standard ΛCDM framework and motivate the explo-
ration of alternative descriptions of cosmic acceleration. From a theoretical perspective, two main
approaches have been developed to explain the accelerated expansion of the Universe. The first re-
tains Einstein’s general relativity and introduces additional dark energy components, while the second
modifies the gravitational sector itself. Representative modified-gravity scenarios include the Dvali–
Gabadadze–Porrati (DGP) braneworld model [13, 14] andf(R) gravity [15]. Of particular interest is
f(T) gravity [16], which extends the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR). In telepar-
allel gravity, gravitational interactions are described in terms of torsion rather than curvature: the
torsion scalarT, constructed from the curvature-free Weitzenb¨ ock connection, plays a role analogous
to the Ricci scalarRin general relativity. Thef(T) framework generalizes TEGR by replacing the
torsion scalar in the gravitational action with a functionf(T), providing a geometrical mechanism for
generating late-time cosmic acceleration.
Thef(T) framework has attracted considerable attention as a viable alternative description of
cosmic acceleration for several reasons. First, it provides a geometrical mechanism for generating late-
time acceleration without introducing an explicit cosmological constant, thereby offering a different
perspective on the cosmological constant problem [17–20]. Second, some viablef(T) models can
modify the late-time expansion history while remaining close to the standard cosmological evolution
at high redshift, and have therefore been investigated in connection with the Hubble tension [21, 22].
Moreover, the effective dark energy sector induced by modified teleparallel gravity can exhibit a time-
dependent equation of state, including phantom-like behavior or crossing of the phantom divide [19,
23]. From a theoretical perspective, another appealing feature is that the field equations off(T)
gravity remain second order, in contrast to the fourth-order equations generally encountered in metric

f(R) gravity, which simplifies both the theoretical and numerical treatment [19, 24, 25]. More detailed
discussions off(T) gravity can be found in Refs. [16, 19].
The recent baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic
Instrument (DESI) provide a timely opportunity to revisit these modified-gravity scenarios. The DESI
Data Releases 1 and 2 have substantially improved the precision of BAO measurements over a broad
redshift range [26, 27]. In particular, the DESI DR2 analysis, based on the first three years of observa-
tions and more than 14 million galaxies and quasars, provides significantly improved measurements of
the cosmic distance–redshift relation. When combined with cosmic microwave background (CMB) and
Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) observations, the DESI BAO measurements strengthen the preference
for an evolving dark energy equation of state within parametrized dynamical dark energy models,
although the significance depends on the adopted SNe Ia sample and model parameterization [28–33].
Although parametrized dynamical dark energy models, such as the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder
(CPL) parameterization, provide a flexible description of departures from a cosmological constant,
they remain phenomenological and do not by themselves specify the underlying physical mechanism
responsible for cosmic acceleration. In this context, confronting viablef(T) gravity models with
the latest DESI DR2 measurements provides a useful test of whether the indications of departures
from ΛCDM found in phenomenological dark energy analyses can also be accommodated within a
modified-gravity framework. Several studies have begun to confrontf(T) gravity with the DESI DR2
measurements. In Ref. [34], the power-law, Linder, and exponentialf(T) models were constrained
using DESI DR2 BAO together with cosmic chronometers, DES Year 5 SNe Ia, and CMB distance
priors. The analysis showed that deviations allowed by late-time observations are substantially reduced
after the CMB information is included. More recently, Ref. [35] investigated severalf(T) models using
Pantheon+, DESI DR2 BAO, CMB distance priors, and redshift-space distortion measurements, with
particular emphasis on theH 0 andS 8 tensions and the growth of cosmic structure. These studies
demonstrate the constraining power of recent observations, while also motivating a closer comparison
of how different distance probes affect the inferred model parameters. In particular, a displacement of a
marginalized posterior away from the ΛCDM limit does not necessarily imply that the extended model
is statistically preferred. The location and width of the posterior characterize parameter constraints
within a model, whereas comparisons of the maximum likelihood and information criteria address
its performance relative to a reference model. It is therefore useful to examine these complementary
aspects together when assessing the response off(T) models to different combinations of cosmological
observations.

3 Data and Methodology

4 Results and Discussion

sidered in this work. Recent analyses combining DESI BAO with other cosmological observations
have reported indications of departures from the ΛCDM model within dynamical dark energy scenar-

DR2 BAO measurements in the framework off(T) gravity. Then, we examine how the correspond-
ing parameter regions change when the CMB distance priors and the Pantheon+ SNe Ia sample are
progressively included. It also allows us to examine whether the indication of a deviation from the
ΛCDM model remains in the combined analysis and whether the result depends on the specificf(T)
model. We further investigate the transition redshiftz T to characterize the onset of late-time cosmic
acceleration and assess the relative statistical performance of the models using the Akaike information
criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian information criterion (BIC).

5 Conclusions
Recent analyses based on DESI observations have reported indications of departures from the ΛCDM
model within dynamical dark energy scenarios. In this work, we investigate whether similar behavior
appears in three representativef(T) cosmological models using the DESI DR2 BAO measurements,
together with the CMB distance priors and Pantheon+ SNe Ia data. Our main conclusions can be
summarized as follows.
First, the evidence for a deviation from the ΛCDM model is strongly dependent on the specific
form of thef(T) model and on the adopted data combination. For thef 1CDM model, the mild
deviation found from BAO+CMB disappears after the Pantheon+ SNe Ia data are included, and the
full combination is consistent with the ΛCDM model. In contrast, thef 2CDM model favors a positive
and nonzero value of the deformation parameterbin the full analysis. Thef 3CDM model shows an
even stronger dependence on the data combination, with a substantial change in the allowedn–Ω m
parameter space after the CMB distance priors are included. These results show that the departures
from the ΛCDM model suggested by DESI analyses are not reproduced in a universal manner inf(T)
gravity.
Second, a deviation from the ΛCDM model at the parameter level does not necessarily imply a
statistical preference for the corresponding modified gravity model. This is most clearly illustrated by
thef 2CDM model. Although the full BAO+CMB+SN analysis favors a nonzero value ofband gives a
slightly lower minimumχ2 than the ΛCDM model, the improvement is not sufficient to establish a clear
model selection preference once the additional degree of freedom is taken into account. Thef 1CDM
model also shows no statistical advantage over the ΛCDM model, while thef 3CDM model is more
strongly disfavored by the combined data. The parameter constraints and information criterion results
therefore provide complementary information when assessing possible departures from the standard
cosmological model.
Third, the transition redshift remains comparatively stable despite the different behaviors of the
model parameters. The threef(T) models generally givez T values within a relatively narrow range
and broadly consistent with the reference constraints from Planck 2018 and Pantheon sample. Thus,
the differences in the modified gravity parameters do not lead to comparably large changes in the
inferred epoch at which the Universe transitions from decelerated to accelerated expansion.
The present analysis is based primarily on traditional observational datasets. A more complete
test off(T) gravity will require observables that are directly sensitive to the growth of cosmic struc-
ture and gravitational perturbations. Future studies using independent late-time probes, such as
gravitational-wave standard sirens, strong gravitational lensing, and fast radio bursts, together with
observables sensitive to the growth of cosmic structure, will provide complementary tests off(T) grav-
ity and help further distinguish between different model parameterizations. The increasing precision
of upcoming large-scale-structure surveys will therefore provide an important opportunity to further
test modified gravity as an alternative explanation of the late-time cosmic acceleration.
