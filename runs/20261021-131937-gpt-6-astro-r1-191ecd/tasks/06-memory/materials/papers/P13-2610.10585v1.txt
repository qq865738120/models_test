Partially Thermalized eV-Scale Sterile Neutrinos in Dynamical Dark Energy and
Non-Flat Cosmologies
Gopal Kashyap 1,∗ and Kanhaiya Lal Pandey 1,†
1Department of Physics, School of Advanced Sciences,

(Dated: October 9, 2026)
We investigate cosmological constraints on a (3 + 1) neutrino framework containing an additional
partially thermalized, eV-scale sterile neutrino alongside the three active species. Rather than
assuming full thermalization, we allow the sterile abundance to vary independently through its
physical massm s and contribution ∆Neff to the effective number of relativistic species. The active-
neutrino sector is analyzed separately for normal and inverted mass ordering, while the sterile state
is modeled as a thermally distributed non-cold relic with a temperature below that of the active-
neutrino background. We consider flat and non-flat ΛCDM andw0waCDM cosmologies, all including
the same (3+1)νsector. The models are constrained using Planck 2018 CMB data, DESI DR2 BAO,
Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae, a conservative compilation off σ 8 measurements, and the recent
Hubble Distance Network constraint,H 0 = 73.50±0.81 km s −1 Mpc−1. Oscillation-informed priors
are imposed on the active-neutrino mass splittings, while the eV scale sterile-mass range is motivated
by short-baseline oscillation searches and global (3 + 1) analyses. We find that allowing additional
freedom in the late-time cosmological background substantially changes the inferred sterile-neutrino
sector. The effective sterile mass increases fromm s,eff ∼0.11±0.05 eV in flat ΛCDM+(3 + 1)νto
∼0.35±0.12 eV in the non-flatw 0waCDM+(3 + 1)νmodel. The corresponding sterile abundance
shifts from ∆N eff ≲0.1 to ∆N eff ≃0.35±0.15, while remaining well below the fully thermalized
limit, ∆N eff ≃1. A direct comparison with the induced prior onm s,eff shows that the posterior
displacement toward larger effective sterile masses is not solely a consequence of transformed prior
volume. The non-flatw 0waCDM models also show∼2.8σpreference for open spatial geometry,
Ωk ≃0.0045±0.0016, and yieldH 0 ≃70.9±0.7 km s −1 Mpc−1 andS 8 ≃0.785±0.015. These
models improve the minimumχ2 by approximately 21-25 relative to flat ΛCDM+(3+1)ν. Our results
therefore show that the cosmological viability of a partially thermalized eV-scale sterile neutrino is
strongly correlated with assumptions about spatial curvature and the late-time dark-energy sector.
I. INTRODUCTION
Neutrino oscillation experiments have established that
neutrinos are massive and that the three active flavor
states are mixtures of mass eigenstates, providing one
of the clearest indications of physics beyond the min-
imal Standard Model [1–3]. The possibility of addi-
tional sterile neutrino states, which do not participate
directly in standard weak interactions, has been ex-
tensively investigated in connection with anomalies re-
ported by short-baseline (SBL) experiments, including
LSND and MiniBooNE [4, 5]. Reactor, accelerator, and
other disappearance measurements, such as, Daya Bay,
MINOS/MINOS+, and IceCube have subsequently pro-
vided important constraints on the corresponding ac-
tive–sterile mixing parameters, while also highlighting
persistent tensions among different experimental datasets
[6–8]. Neutrinos provide an important link between par-
ticle physics and cosmology, with their masses and rel-
ativistic energy density leaving characteristic imprints
on the expansion history, cosmic microwave background
(CMB), and large-scale structure [9–12]. A sterile neu-
trino with a mass at the eV scale is particularly inter-

esting cosmologically because, if sufficiently populated in
the early Universe, it contributes to the relativistic en-
ergy density around recombination and later behaves as
a component of hot dark matter. Cosmological observa-
tions therefore provide a complementary probe of light
sterile-neutrino scenarios, sensitive to both their abun-
dance and their impact on the expansion history and
growth of cosmic structure.
The cosmological implications of sterile neutrinos de-
pend crucially on their production history and phase-
space distribution. In the simplest scenario, a fully ther-
malized sterile neutrino contributes approximately one
additional effective neutrino species, ∆Neff ≃1, while its
non-relativistic energy density is determined by its phys-
ical mass. Such a scenario is strongly constrained by
combinations of cosmic microwave background (CMB)
and large-scale-structure observations [13–15]. In partic-
ular, the Planck 2018 analysis found stringent constraints
on both the effective number of relativistic species and
the effective mass of an additional sterile relic, strongly
disfavoring a fully thermalized eV-scale sterile neutrino
when combined with baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO)
data [13]. These constraints, however, should not be in-
terpreted as model-independent exclusions of sterile neu-
trinos, since the cosmological abundance of a sterile state
need not correspond to complete thermalization. Mecha-
nisms such as suppressed production, non-standard neu-
trino interactions, or a modified thermal history can

lead to a reduced sterile abundance and consequently to

An important recent development in this direction is
the systematic comparison of different sterile-neutrino
production scenarios. Ladeira et al. [17] considered
fully thermalized, different-temperature thermal relic,
and Dodelson–Widrow-like realizations and showed that
current cosmological data place substantially stronger
constraints on the effective sterile abundance than on its
physical mass when the sterile population is suppressed.
In particular, their different-temperature sterile scenario
remains considerably less constrained than the fully ther-
malized case, emphasizing the importance of distinguish-
ing the physical sterile mass from its cosmological abun-
dance. This result is consistent with the broader pic-
ture in which cosmological observations constrain the en-
ergy density and free-streaming effects of relic neutrinos
rather than directly measuring their microscopic produc-
tion mechanism. The distinction between physical and
effective sterile-neutrino masses has also been incorpo-
rated in earlier Planck analyses of massive sterile relics
[13]. Thus, a meaningful assessment of eV-scale sterile
neutrinos requires the thermalization level and the phys-
ical mass to be treated as separate quantities.
The interpretation of neutrino cosmology is further
complicated by degeneracies with the late-time expan-
sion history. Massive neutrinos alter the background
evolution and suppress the growth of matter perturba-
tions through free streaming, while changes in the dark-
energy equation of state can partially compensate these
effects in cosmological observables [18–24]. More recent
analyses using DESI BAO data have likewise emphasized
the sensitivity of neutrino-mass constraints to assump-
tions about the dark-energy sector, with evolving dark
energy providing additional freedom in accommodating
the cosmological neutrino-mass scale [25–28]. This is-
sue is particularly relevant in light of the DESI BAO
measurements, which have provided indications of depar-
tures from a cosmological constant when combined with
complementary distance measurements [29–31]. Con-
sequently, sterile-neutrino constraints obtained within
ΛCDM may not fully characterize the allowed parameter
space when the expansion history is generalized [28, 32].
The increasing precision of late-time cosmological ob-
servations makes it possible to investigate these effects
using complementary probes. The Planck CMB mea-
surements tightly constrain the early-Universe radia-
tion content and neutrino-induced changes to the CMB
anisotropies [13], while BAO measurements provide pre-
cise information about the distance-redshift relation and
the expansion history. The recent DESI DR2 BAO mea-
surements have substantially improved the precision of
cosmological constraints and have also highlighted the
interplay between neutrino masses and dynamical dark
energy [31]. Type Ia supernovae provide an independent
probe of the late-time expansion history, while redshift-
space-distortion measurements constrain the growth of
structure throughf σ 8. Combining these probes is
therefore particularly useful for separating the effects
of sterile-neutrino abundance, neutrino free streaming,
dark-energy dynamics, and spatial curvature. In addi-
tion, local measurements of the Hubble constant provide
a complementary constraint on the late-time expansion
rate and can alter the allowed degeneracies betweenN eff,
neutrino masses, and dark energy.
In this work, we investigate a (3 + 1) neutrino frame-
work containing three active neutrinos and an additional
partially thermalized, eV-scale sterile neutrino. The ster-
ile sector is characterized independently by its physical
mass and its contribution ∆N eff to the effective num-
ber of relativistic species, and is modeled as a thermal
non-cold relic with a temperature lower than that of
the active-neutrino background. This allows us to ex-
plore the sterile-neutrino parameter space continuously
between strongly suppressed and fully populated regimes
while consistently accounting for the transition of the
sterile state from relativistic to non-relativistic behav-
ior. We consider both normal and inverted ordering
(hereafter NO and IO) of the active-neutrino masses
and investigate a sequence of cosmological models rang-
ing from flat ΛCDM to flatw 0waCDM and spatially
curvedw 0waCDM, the latter two models are studied in-
dependently for NO and IO both. Our analysis com-
bines Planck 2018 CMB data, DESI DR2 BAO mea-
surements, Pantheon+ Type Ia supernovae, conservative
f σ8 measurements, and a GaussianH 0 prior from the
recent Hubble Distance Network measurement. We ad-
ditionally incorporate neutrino-oscillation adapted from
recent JUNO [33], and earlier short-baseline (SBL) ex-
periments [34]. Within this framework, we quantify the
joint constraints on the sterile-neutrino mass and ther-
malization level and examine their degeneracies with the
active-neutrino mass sector, dark-energy dynamics, and
spatial curvature. This enables us to assess whether the
cosmological viability of an eV-scale sterile neutrino re-
mains substantially altered once partial thermalization
and extensions of the late-time cosmological model are
simultaneously taken into account.
The paper is organized as follows. Section II introduces
the cosmological and neutrino framework, including the
active-neutrino mass ordering, the sterile-neutrino sector,
the background cosmology, and the growth of structure.
Section III describes the observational data sets used in
the analysis. The statistical methodology, including the
parameter priors, numerical implementation, and model-
comparison criteria, is presented in Sec. IV. The resulting
cosmological constraints are discussed in Sec. V, and our
main conclusions are summarized in Sec. VI.

enue toward determining the neutrino mass ordering. In
our analysis, we incorporate this improved oscillation in-
formation through priors on the active-neutrino mass-
squared splittings motivated by the recent JUNO mea-
surements.

IV. STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
A. Model space and parameter priors
We consider four cosmological model classes, summa-
rized in Table III, each analyzed separately for normal
and inverted ordering of the three active-neutrino masses.
All models share the same (3+1)νsector, while the late-
time background is varied through the dark-energy and
spatial-curvature sectors.
The flat ΛCDM+(3 + 1)νmodel serves as the refer-
ence cosmology, withw=−1 and Ω k = 0. We then
consider the flat CPL+(3 + 1)νextension, in which the
dark-energy equation of state is allowed to evolve, and
the ΛCDM+Ωk + (3 + 1)νmodel, which retainsw=−1
while allowing spatial curvature to vary. Finally, we con-
sider the CPL+Ω k + (3 + 1)νmodel, in which both dy-
namical dark energy and spatial curvature are free. The
ΛCDM+Ωk+(3+1)νcase provides a useful control model
for isolating the effect of curvature from that of CPL dy-
namics.

B. Numerical implementation and likelihood
The background evolution and linear perturbations are
calculated usingCLASS. A customCLASS-Cobayainter-
face maps (m lightest, m s,∆N eff,ordering) onto the four
physical non-cold relic masses and temperatures before
each Boltzmann evaluation.
Posterior sampling is performed withCobayausing
multiple parallel Markov chains and adaptive proposal
learning. Each cosmological model and active-neutrino
mass ordering is sampled independently. Convergence
is monitored using the Gelman-Rubin statistic, and the
final chains are required to satisfyR−1≲0.03. For
the fiducial Pantheon+ data combinationD PP defined in
Eq. (19), the total log-likelihood is
lnL tot = lnL CMB + lnL BAO + lnL PP
+ lnL f σ8 + lnL H0 .(21)
Marginalized posterior distributions and credible in-
tervals are obtained from the converged chains using
GetDist[58]. The quoted two-sided uncertainties corre-
spond to 68% marginalized credible intervals, while one-
sided upper bounds are reported at 95% confidence.

the CPL and CPL+Ω k extensions, and subsequently use
the ΛCDM+Ωk model to disentangle the effects of spatial
curvature from those of dynamical dark energy.

VI. SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK
We have investigated a partially thermalized sterile-
neutrino scenario in a 3+1 framework, allowing simul-
taneously for dynamical dark energy and spatial curva-
ture. We considered both normal and inverted ordering
of the three active neutrinos and combined CMB, BAO,
Type-Ia supernovae, redshift-space distortion, and local
Hubble-distance measurements. Our results demonstrate
that the cosmological inference of sterile-neutrino proper-
ties depends strongly on both the sterile-neutrino abun-
dance and the assumed late-time expansion history.
A central result of this work is that allowing partial
thermalization permits an eV-scale sterile-neutrino sec-
tor to remain compatible with the cosmological data con-
sidered here. Rather than assuming a fully thermalized
fourth species, we allow its relativistic contribution ∆Neff
and physical mass to vary independently, thereby ac-
counting for a suppressed sterile abundance. We find
∆Neff ≃0.24 in the flat CPL models and≃0.34-0.36 in
the non-flat CPL models, substantially below the fully
thermalized limit ∆N eff ≃1. Correspondingly, the ef-
fective sterile mass increases fromm s,eff ≃0.09-0.11 eV
in flat ΛCDM to 0.19-0.23 eV in flat CPL and≃0.35
eV in the non-flat CPL models. Thus, the extended cos-
mologies permit a substantially larger sterile contribution
while retaining a subthermal sterile abundance.
This result is particularly relevant in light of the recent
analysis of Ladeira et al., [17] which explicitly examined
whether cosmological data exclude sterile neutrinos in
general or primarily the fully thermalized scenario. Using
Planck, DESI DR2 BAO and supernova data, they found
that the fully thermalized sterile scenario is strongly dis-
favored, whereas partially populated sterile-neutrino re-
alizations remain viable, their different-temperature ster-
ile relic provided the least cosmological tension among
the cases considered. They further showed that cos-
mological data constrain the effective sterile abundance
much more strongly than the physical sterile mass [17].
Our results are consistent with this broader picture, but
extend it by allowing the sterile abundance to vary con-
tinuously within a (3+1) model while simultaneously
varying both dark-energy dynamics and spatial curva-
ture. In particular, our non-flat CPL models accommo-
datem s,eff ≃0.35 eV with ∆N eff ≃0.35, illustrating
explicitly how a moderately populated sterile species can
leave a larger effective mass signature without approach-
ing the fully thermalized limit.
Our results agree with recent DESI DR2 analyses
showing that the inclusion of additional radiation can
substantially weaken cosmological constraints on sterile-
neutrino masses, allowing partially thermalized sub-eV
sterile neutrinos to remain viable when combined with
a localH 0 measurement [64]. Our results also agree
qualitatively with other recent DESI analyses. For ex-
ample, Du et al. found that aw 0waCDM model with
a sterile neutrino can accommodate a nonzero effec-
tive sterile mass at approximately the 2σlevel, obtain-
ingm s,eff = 0.50 +0.33
−0.27 eV, whereas their corresponding
ΛCDM and constant-w models did not provide evidence
for a massive sterile neutrino [65]. Our inferred values
are somewhat lower,m s,eff ≃0.35 eV in the non-flat CPL
models, but exhibit the same qualitative dependence on
the freedom in the late-time expansion history. The dif-
ference in the numerical constraints is expected given the
different datasets, treatment of the sterile distribution,
active-neutrino sector, and inclusion of spatial curvature.
Allowing extensions of the late-time cosmology also re-
laxes the active-neutrino mass constraints. The total
active mass increases from 0.0707 +0.0016
−0.0011 eV (NO) and

−0.0097 eV (IO) in flat ΛCDM to 0.0982 +0.0059

and 0.1315 +0.0064

models. At the same time,H 0 increases from∼69
km/s/Mpc in flat ΛCDM to∼70.9 km/s/Mpc in the
non-flat CPL models. These shifts illustrate the strong
degeneracy between neutrino masses, the sterile abun-
dance, and the late-time expansion history. They are
also consistent with the broader DESI BAO finding that
neutrino-mass constraints can be substantially modified
when the assumption of a cosmological constant is re-
laxed [27, 31, 66, 67]. Our results further demonstrate
that this behavior persists in an enlarged (3+1) neutrino
sector, where the active-neutrino mass budget is accom-
panied by a partially thermalized sterile component.
The model-comparison statistics further demonstrate
the impact of these extensions. Relative to flat
ΛCDM(3+1)ν, the CPL models improve the minimum
χ2 by 13.6-15.8, while the non-flat CPL models improve
it by 21.1-24.8. The corresponding AIC differences are
negative, ∆AIC=-9.6 to -18.8, and the Bayesian evidence
increases, with lnB=+1.0 to +8.2. The BIC provides a
more conservative assessment, particularly for the CPL
models. Thus, while the extended models substantially

improve the fit, the statistical evidence depends on the
adopted complexity penalty and does not independently
establish the necessity of curvature or dynamical dark
energy.
The comparison between the normal and inverted
active-neutrino orderings reveals a non-negligible depen-
dence of the cosmological fit on the assumed mass hier-
archy. In our analysis, the inverted-ordering models con-
sistently yield larger improvements in the minimumχ 2,
as well as more favorable AIC values and larger Bayesian
evidence ratios, than their normal-ordering counterparts.
This trend is seen across both the flat and non-flat
dynamical-dark-energy models. This hierarchy depen-
dence is noteworthy in light of recent cosmological anal-
yses showing that the inferred neutrino-mass constraints
and ordering preference can depend appreciably on the
assumed hierarchy, data combination, and cosmologi-
cal model [68, 69]. At the same time, recent DESI-
based analyses within the standard three-neutrino frame-
work have reported a preference for the normal ordering
[66, 70], while global oscillation analyses provide an in-
dependent, although not yet overwhelming, sensitivity to
the mass ordering [71]. However, our BIC results, provide
a more nuanced picture, although the inverted-ordering
models have smaller BIC differences than the correspond-
ing normal-ordering models, the positive ∆BIC values
for the CPL extensions indicate that the additional de-
grees of freedom are not uniformly favored once the BIC
penalty is taken into account. Thus, the apparent pref-
erence for the inverted ordering in our extended models
should not be interpreted as an independent cosmolog-
ical determination of the mass hierarchy, but rather as
a model-dependent outcome of the combined fit and the
adopted model-selection criterion.
Finally, our results are relevant to the recent discus-
sion of an apparent cosmological preference for an effec-
tive neutrino mass below the physical lower bound [72–
75]. Recent analyses have shown that this preference can
be sensitive to CMB lensing and to the assumed late-
time cosmology, with dynamical dark energy shifting the
inferred effective mass toward physically allowed values
[74, 76–79]. Our analysis instead keeps all physical neu-
trino masses non-negative and explores how additional
freedom in (w 0, wa,Ω k), together with a partially pop-
ulated sterile sector, modifies the cosmological neutrino
constraints. Overall, our results indicate that current
cosmological data do not generically exclude the presence
of an eV-scale sterile neutrino, but instead place strong
constraints on its degree of thermalization and effective
cosmological abundance. Future CMB-lensing, BAO, su-
pernova, and large-scale-structure measurements will be
crucial for determining whether the present indications
for a nonzero sterile contribution persist and for disentan-
gling sterile-neutrino physics from dark-energy dynamics
and spatial curvature.
