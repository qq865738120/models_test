EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS OF VISCOUS-DISPERSIVE SHOCK
PROFILES FOR THE NAVIER–STOKES–FOURIER–KORTEWEG SYSTEM
SUNGHO HAN
Abstract.We establish the existence and uniqueness, up to translation, of monotone viscous-
dispersive shock profiles for the one-dimensional Navier–Stokes–Fourier–Korteweg (NSFK) system,
provided the two constant end states satisfy the Rankine–Hugoniot and Lax entropy conditions and
the shock strength is small. Treating the small shock strength as a singular perturbation parameter,
we construct the profiles via Fenichel’s geometric singular perturbation theory. We further provide
pointwise estimates for the profiles, which are used in the nonlinear stability analysis of generic
Riemann solutions to the NSFK system in the companion work [22].

1.1.Literature Review and Motivation.For the barotropic Navier–Stokes–Korteweg (NSK)
system, the existence theory is developed in [12, 27, 4, 21, 1]. Nonlinear stability is known for
rarefaction waves [7], viscous-dispersive shock profiles [8, 23], and composite waves of a rarefaction
wave and a viscous-dispersive shock profile [24]. Spectral stability of viscous-dispersive shock
profiles in Korteweg-type and quantum-hydrodynamic models is investigated in [19, 20].
For the full NSFK system, the existence theory is developed in [28, 26, 9, 29], and stability is
known for a single viscous contact wave [11] and for a composite wave of two rarefaction waves
and a viscous contact wave [10]. The companion work [22] treats a composite wave of a rarefaction
wave, a viscous contact wave, and a viscous-dispersive shock profile, as the NSFK analogue of the
result of Kang–Vasseur–Wang [34] for the full Navier–Stokes–Fourier (NSF) system.
The analysis in [22] is based on the method ofa-contraction with shifts introduced by Kang and
Vasseur [32, 31] and extended to composite waves by Kang–Vasseur–Wang [33]. The method has
been applied to viscous-dispersive shocks of the NSK system [23, 24, 25, 17] and of KdV–Burgers-
type equations [5, 6, 16]. It requires a monotone viscous-dispersive shock profile satisfying pointwise
estimates, and the companion work [22] uses these estimates with constants uniform for left end
states in a compact set.
To construct such profiles for the NSFK system, we use Fenichel’s geometric singular perturbation
theory [18]. See also [30]. The same framework was applied by Eo–Eun–Kang–Oh [15] and Eo and
Eun [14] to establish the existence and uniqueness of viscous shock profiles for the Brenner–Navier–
Stokes–Fourier (BNSF) system and to derive pointwise estimates. Related slow-manifold analyses
of small-amplitude viscous-dispersive profiles appear in [23, 19, 20]. Compared with the barotropic
problem, the full NSFK profile equations combine a temperature-dependent pressure and heat
conduction with capillarity, leading to additional nonlinear coupling.
Another issue concerns whether viscous-dispersive shock profiles are monotone or have oscilla-
tory tails. For the scalar Korteweg–de Vries–Burgers (KdVB) equation, Bona and Schonbek [3]
characterized the transition between the two behaviors. The stability of monotone KdVB profiles
is studied in [36, 5], and the structure and stability of oscillatory KdVB profiles in [6]. For the
modified KdVB equation, whose flux is non-convex, quantitative properties of monotone degener-
ate shock profiles and the stability of their composite waves with a rarefaction wave are obtained
in [16]. At the system level, Smoller and Shapiro [37] obtained early existence results under con-
stant viscosity and dispersion matrices, and Folino–Lattanzio–Plaza [19] established the existence
and uniqueness up to translation of viscous-dispersive shock profiles of arbitrary amplitude for the
barotropic NSK system. The profiles constructed here for the NSFK system are monotone for fixed
positiveµ, α, κand small shock strength.
