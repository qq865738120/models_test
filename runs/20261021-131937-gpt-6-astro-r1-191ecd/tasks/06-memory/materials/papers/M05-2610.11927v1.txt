Uniqueness of pressureless flow
Ryan Hynd∗ and Hwa Kil Kim †
October 9, 2026
Abstract
We consider the pressureless Euler equations in one spatial dimension. These equa-
tions model the dynamics of particles on a line that interact only through perfectly
inelastic collisions. The flow was first shown to be unique for given initial conditions
by Huang and Wang, assuming the entropy and the strong initial continuity of energy
conditions hold. In this paper, we prove an analogous uniqueness theorem using a
Lagrangian interpretation. The key ingredients are showing that each solution sat-
isfying the entropy condition and an initial kinetic energy bound has this particular
type of Lagrangian interpretation and a monotonicity inequality involving conditional
expectation.

Our contribution.Our aim is to give a direct approach to the uniqueness question
studied by Huang and Wang in 2001 [11]. Huang and Wang’s result allows more general
initial mass measuresρ 0. However, their proof required bounded initial velocitiesv 0. Here
we assume thatρ 0 has a finite second moment but only supposev 0 ∈L 2(ρ0).
Uniqueness of solutions to the pressureless Euler equations for given initial conditions
remains of interest. Recently, Suder and Tudorascu [21] use the Huang and Wang uniqueness
criteria to identify the sticky particle flows considered there with scalar conservation law
solutions for bounded, right-continuous initial velocities. Likewise, Carrillo and Galtung [4]
established equivalence between gradient flow solutions and entropy solutions of associated
scalar conservation laws for pressureless Euler systems, including systems with interaction
forces.
As mentioned above, we derive a Lagrangian coordinate from every weak solution pair
satisfying the entropy condition and initial kinetic energy bound and prove uniqueness us-
ing (1.12). Our proof is independent of earlier uniqueness results. We anticipate that this
method may also be useful for pressureless Euler systems with interaction potentials.
This paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we recall a few key properties
of weak solutions and prove the strong initial continuity of energy. We also recall some
properties of conditional expectation and prove inequality (1.12). In Section 3, we show that
corresponding to any weak solution pair of (1.1) is a Borel probability measureηon the space
of continuous functions from [0,∞) intoRwith the topology of local uniform convergence.
Then we show in Section 4 that thisηis determined by anXwhich solves (1.11). Finally,
we will use this observation to prove the Uniqueness Theorem in Section 5.
