Abstract.Robin’s inequality reformulates the Riemann hypothesis as a bound on the sum-of-
divisors functionσ. We introduce a finite-truncation version of Robin’s inequality determined
intrinsically by the arithmetic structure ofn,

rigid extremal class of integers.
1.Introduction
In [Rob84], Robin conjectured the inequality (calledRobin’s inequality)σ(n)< e γnln lnn, for
alln >5040, whereσ(n) is the sum-of-divisors function ofn, andγ= 0.577215...is the Euler-
Mascheroni constant, and proved that it is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis (RH). Earlier it
was shown by Ramanujan that RH itself implies this inequality [Ram15], albeit the asymptotic
form, and hence this inequality is also called theRamanujan-Robin criterion[Bro17, Chapter 7].
Since then, this inequality has been unconditionally proven for many classes of integers [LS25].
These include: odd integers greater than 9 and square–free integers greater than 30 [CLMS07];
integers greater than 720 expressible as a sum of two squares [BHMWN09]; integers free ofp 5
[CLMS07],p 7 [SP12],p 11 [BT15],p 20 [PM21], andp 21 [Axl23] divisors for any primep.

(i)Unconditional results.We prove that theω−inequality (2) holds unconditionally for all

We further obtain quantitative bounds on its multiplicities, reducing the search at fixed

6.Discussion
Theω-inequality gives a finite-truncation criterion equivalent to Robin’s inequality and hence
to the Riemann hypothesis. More generally, any integer-valued truncationA(n) in (1) satisfying
A(n)≥ω(n)
lies between theω-inequality and Robin’s inequality and is therefore again equivalent to RH; the
π-inequality is one example.
Theω-inequality arose from an extension of the study of inequalities in [MMS23] and was first
supported by limited numerical evidence. The results developed here were obtained in stages.
We first established the unconditional cases (primorials in Theorem 1, square-free integers in
Corollary 9, and odd integers in Corollary 10) and the multiplicity bounds for a hypothetical
minimal counterexample (Lemma 15), except for the final conditiona K = 1 derived in Theorem 2.
Provinga K = 1 led to a proof of equivalence with Robin’s inequality (Theorem 4). The resultaK =
1 remains important for a different reason: for each fixedK, it reduces the relevant search to a finite
set of multiplicity tuples. This finite reduction, together with bounds for the truncated exponential
that also entered the equivalence proof, is used in proving that a minimal counterexample must
be superabundant.
The scaleω(n) is also close to a natural lower threshold. Indeed, replacingω(n) byω(n)−1
already gives infinitely many counterexamples. Forn= 2 a one hasA(n) =ω(n)−1 = 0, so the
corresponding right-hand side ise γ, whereas
σ(2a)
2a = 2− 1

for everya≥3. Thus every 2 a >5040 is a counterexample. The same argument applies to
A(n) =⌊c ω(n)⌋for every fixed 0< c <1, sinceA(2 a) = 0. Hence no uniform proportional
reduction of the truncation order is possible, although this does not establish pointwise minimality
ofω(n) among all arithmetic truncation rules.
A further consequence of the present analysis is that, if theω-inequality fails, its minimal
counterexample is superabundant. We record this in Theorem 3. Hence it is sufficient to verify the
inequality on superabundant integers, or using Corollary 24, on the even smaller class of colossally
abundant integers. This places the truncated criterion within the same extremal framework that
governs maximal values ofσ(n)/n, while retaining the additional parameterK=ω(n). For fixed
K, the problem is reduced to a finite tuple of non-increasing prime multiplicities subject to the
structural bounds obtained above.
These results suggest two related directions. First, one may ask how far the truncation order
can be reduced while preserving equivalence with Robin’s inequality, and whether there is a natural
minimal arithmetic choice ofA(n). Second, the finite-dimensional problem at fixedKmay admit
a sharper extremal description, for example through explicit optimisation ofσ(n)/nunder size
and multiplicity constraints, or through a sparse extremal sequence adapted specifically to the
ω-truncation. Such a reduction would go beyond the equivalence itself and may clarify the ways
in which the finite-truncation formulation provides additional leverage on Robin’s criterion.
