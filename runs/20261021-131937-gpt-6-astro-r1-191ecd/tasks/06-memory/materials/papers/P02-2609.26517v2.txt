Addressing the Phantom Crossing by Local limit of Nonlocal Gravity

The second data release of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), based on more than fourteen
million galaxies and quasars, prefers a dark energy component whose equation of state is dynamical. In the
simplest two-parameter description of this behavior, the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) parametrization, the
data favor the quadrantw 0 >−1,w a <0, which implies that the equation of state crosses the phantom divide
w=−1at a redshiftz≃0.4. We show that the local limit of nonlocal gravity, a teleparallel extension of general
relativity governed by a scalar function called gravitational susceptibilityS(x), offers a natural explanation. In
this model a cosmological constant is not allowed; dark energy is necessarily dynamical. The phantom crossing
seen by an observer is an effective phenomenon generated by the time evolution ofS(z). We establish a
correspondence: to every general-relativistic dark energy model with equation of statew(z), there corresponds a
modified TEGR cosmology withw=−1and a suitable susceptibilityS(z)that are indistinguishable at the level
of the background expansion. We construct the susceptibility functions that reproduce the CPL behavior favored
by DESI data, and we confront the model with the combined CMB,BAO, and SNe data using Boltzmann solver
codes. The recovered susceptibility displays a characteristic low-redshift dip with depthβ=−0.025±0.007
atz≃0.4, and its derivative changes sign at a redshift equal to the phantom-crossing epoch.
I. INTRODUCTION
The standard cosmological model, spatially flatΛCDM,
which combines a cosmological constantΛwith cold dark
matter (CDM), successfully explains a wide range of ob-
servations, most notably the temperature and polarization
anisotropies of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) [1].
Yet the physical nature of dark energy (DE) and dark matter
(DM) remains unknown, and the cosmological constant itself
suffers from severe theoretical problems [2, 3]. It is therefore
not surprising that the search for deviations from cosmologi-
cal constant solution for an accelerating universe has become
one of the main endeavors of observational cosmology today
[4, 5].
The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) was de-
signed precisely for this purpose [6]. It measures the bary-
onic acoustic oscillation (BAO) through the large scale struc-
ture survey. The BAO data alone are described well by a
flatΛCDM model, but the parameters preferred by BAO are
in mild tension with those determined from the CMB: the
ΛCDM fit to DESI+CMB shows a2.3σtension. This tension
disappears when the dark energy equation of state is allowed
to vary with time [7–9].
The statistical preference for dynamical dark energy, and
in particular the exact significance, depends on the data com-
bination and on the assumed parametrization [10–15]. What
matters for the present paper is that the data consistently point
toward a dark energy equation of state that is dynamical and
crossesw=−1at low redshift, known as phantom crossing,
and that this pattern is difficult to realize in general relativity
∗Electronic address: baghram@sharif.edu
with a single field [16–18]. A phantom component is prob-
lematic in general relativity (GR) because it violates the null
energy condition [19, 20]. Modified gravity offers a way out:
the crossing may be an effective description of a modified ex-
pansion history rather than a property of the dark energy fluid
itself [21–27]. Various proposals have been put forward to
accommodate the crossing, ranging from multi-field models
[16, 28] to modified-gravity constructions in which the cross-
ing is only an effective feature [29].
In this paper we show that the local limit of nonlocal grav-
ity (NLG) provides a well-motivated gravitational framework
in which the phantom crossing appears naturally as an effec-
tive phenomenon. NLG is a classical, nonlocal generalization
of GR inspired by the nonlocal electrodynamics of media [30–
32]. This theory is formulated as a modification in the telepar-
allel gravity framework, and its local limit, obtained when the
nonlocal kernel collapses to a delta function, resulting in the
modified teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (modified
TEGR, or MTEGR) model that differs from the base TEGR
by a dimensionless scalar functionS(x), known as the gravi-
tational susceptibility [33].
The cosmological consequences of MTEGR were studied
extensively in [33]. One of the important results of this study
is that out of different standard FLRW models, only the mod-
ified Cartesian flat model is consistent with a time-dependent
susceptibility function, and in this model de Sitter spacetime
is not a valid solution. A cosmological constant is therefore
excluded; instead, dark energy must be dynamical. The mod-
ified continuity equation of this model implies that the energy
density of each component scales with the susceptibility, en-
forcing dynamics on every component, including dark energy
(with, for example,w=−1) [33, 34]. The model was shown
to be capable of improving theH 0 tension [33, 35, 36], and
its linear perturbations were studied and tested with the CMB

II. DESI DR2 AND PHANTOM CROSSING
The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) was de-
signed for the purpose of detecting the deviations of cosmo-
logical expansion from the standardΛCDM model [6]. Its
second data release (DR2) [7] contains spectra of more than
fourteen million galaxies and quasars. From this sample,
the collaboration extracted baryon acoustic oscillation mea-
surements in a number of redshift bins spanning the range
0.1≲z≲4.2, with the highest-redshift bins coming from
the Lyman-αforest [40, 41]. The DR2 BAO data are consis-
tent with the DR1 measurements and with the earlier SDSS re-
sults, and the distance-redshift relation they trace agrees with
that of the recent supernova compilations over the same red-
shift range [7].
The combined DESI+CMB data excludeΛCDM at3.1σ,
and adding supernovae raises the significance to2.8–4.2σde-
pending on the sample used.

A. Data sets
The analysis is based on different data-set permutations
that, according to the DESI DR2 collaboration, most strongly
support dynamical dark energy [7]. Each of these sets plays a
special role in constraining the cosmological parameters, and
we explore multiple supernova catalogs to assess the robust-
ness of our results.
•CMB.We use the final Planck data release (PR4), based
on the NPIPE processing pipeline [1]. Specifically, we
use the full temperature and polarization likelihood, in-
cluding theT T,T E, andEEpower spectra and their
covariance matrices. We refer to this combination col-
lectively asT T T EEE.
•BAO.We use the thirteen BAO measurements from the
second DESI data release (DR2) [7], covering galaxy,
quasar, and Lyman-αtracers. These constitute the most
precise BAO measurements available to date.
•Supernovae.To test the sensitivity of our results to the
choice of supernova catalogue, we perform the analy-
sis using three distinct samples: the Dark Energy Sur-
vey Year-5 (DESY5) sample [44], the Pantheon+ com-
pilation [55], and the Union3 sample [56]. As reported
by the DESI collaboration, the combination involving
the DESY5 sample yields the strongest preference for
a dynamical dark-energy interpretation [7]. Thus, we
focus on this catalogue for comparing different mod-
els. By comparing all three configurations (DESY5,
Pantheon+, and Union3) we can assess whether any in-
ferred deviation fromΛCDM is driven by the choice
of supernova catalogue or represents a robust feature of
the combined data.
B. Numerical tools
As the theoretical platform we use the Boltzmann code
CLASS[57], which we have modified to implement the modi-
fied Cartesian flat model of MTEGR. This includes modifica-
tions in both the background equations (13) and (14) together
with the modified continuity equation in equation (15) and the
linear perturbation equations of Reference [37]. The modi-
fied code is publicly available[63]. For the parameter estima-
tion we use the Monte Carlo Markov Chain samplerCobaya
[58], which interfaces directly with the modifiedCLASS. The
GetDist[59] code has been used to analyze the chains and
to draw triangle posterior plots.
In the code pipeline, the susceptibility functionS(z), and
its derivatives, are treated as input functions of the scale fac-
tor, and the modified continuity equation (equation (15)) fixes
the evolution of all components. The linear perturbation equa-
tions of the modified Cartesian flat model were derived and
validated against the CMB in Reference [37]; we employ them
here without modification, in the synchronous gauge.
The three models we are going to analyze and compare are
CPL, the minimal dynamic dark energy model, EXP, short for
the exponential ansatz in equation (47), and also the standard
ΛCDM model. The CPL and EXP models both have two ad-
ditional parameters compared to the baseΛCDM model.

We now report the results of the MCMC analysis of

Our experiments with multiple functional forms forS(z),

dip, and the dip is the mechanism that generates the phantom
crossing.

VIII. DISCUSSION
The central result of this paper is that the phantom crossing
favored by DESI DR2 can be realized within the local limit
of nonlocal gravity without introducing any phantom com-
ponent. In the modified model, the dark energy fluid has
w=−1at all times and satisfies the modified continuity
equation (equation (15)); the model does not introduce an ex-
plicit phantom matter degree of freedom. What crosses−1is
the effective equation of state (equation (43)), a derived quan-
tity that encodes the difference between the true expansion
history of the modified model and the standard Friedmann in-
terpretation of that history. This interpretation has a number
of appealing features. First, it avoids the well-known instabili-
ties and fine-tuning problems of phantom fields [16]. Second,
it makes the crossing generic: any susceptibility with a dip
produces a crossing, and the data select a dip.
The physical content of the crossing is the dip in the sus-
ceptibilityS(z)atz≃0.4, which represents a transient,
low-redshift feature in the gravitational response of spacetime.
This behavior is qualitatively analogous to the frequency-
dependent response of a medium in electrodynamics: near a
resonance, the real part of a material response function can ex-

Several other frameworks have been proposed to explain
the DESI DR2 phantom crossing. In scalar-tensor gravity, a
reconstruction of the underlying Lagrangian from the DESI
DR2 data was performed in Reference [29], where the cross-
ing is realized through a non-minimal coupling and a modi-
fied effective gravitational constant; in that model the inferred
H0 is also increased relative toΛCDM. In quintom models,
the crossing is achieved with two fields, one quintessence-like
and one phantom-like [16]. In string-inspired settings, self-
dual potentials have been proposed that mimic the DESI DR2
behavior with a single rolling field near a maximum of its po-
tential [62]. The present framework differs from all of these in
an essential way: no new matter component, no second field,
and no phantom field is introduced. The crossing is a property
of the gravitational sector itself, specifically of the suscepti-
bilityS(z), whose role is analogous to that of a constitutive
function of a medium. In this respect the model is closer in
spirit to the electrodynamics-of-media analogy that motivated
nonlocal gravity in the first place [30, 31].
