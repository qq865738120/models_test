Polynomial-time simulation of non-Clifford quantum error correction
S,erban Cercelescu,1,∗Mark Koch,1,†and Arthur Pesah2,‡
1Department of Computer Science, University of Oxford, Oxford, UK
2Dahlem Center for Complex Quantum Systems,
Freie Universität Berlin, 14195 Berlin, Germany
We show that every intermediate state of a broad class of non-Clifford quantum error-correction
circuits is a third-order phase-polynomial state, even under stochastic Pauli noise. The class includes
magic state distillation and cultivation, code switching and gauge fixing, transversal non-Clifford
gates with syndrome extraction, and injection of diagonal magic states. Third-order phase-polynomial
states strictly generalize stabilizer states, and we prove that they are exactly the stabilizer states
of the diagonal-Clifford-and-Pauli (DCP) stabilizer formalism that we introduce. We characterize
these states and show that their representations can be updated in polynomial time. This yields an
exact polynomial-time simulation algorithm for these circuits and, more broadly, a framework for
reasoning about their internal states and about magic states in general. We provide an open-source
implementation, merlin, and benchmark it against existing non-Clifford simulators on distillation,
cultivation, and code switching circuits. The benchmarks demonstrate improved runtime and memory
scaling as the number of logical outputs grows in Bravyi-Haah distillation, and simulation of a code
switching circuit beyond the reach of all other tested simulators.

I. INTRODUCTION
Designing a fault-tolerant quantum architecture re-
quires choosing an error-correcting code, a syndrome-
extraction procedure, and protocols for implementing
logical gates. Comparing these choices often requires
circuit-level simulation under physically motivated noise
models, since their logical error rates and thresholds are
generally difficult to determine analytically. Efficient
Clifford simulation tools, such asstim [1], have made it
possible to evaluate and compare a wide range of quantum
memory experiments and logical Clifford operations.
Extending this capability to non-Clifford operations,
which are required for universal quantum computation,
remains a challenge. Numerous fault-tolerant protocols
have been proposed, either to implement such operations
directly or to prepare encodedmagic statesthat enable
them through state injection. These include magic state
distillation [2–5], cultivation [6, 7], code switching [8–11],
and pieceable fault tolerance [12]. Recent constructions
havestrengthenedtheprospectsforpreparingmagicstates
at high rates, including constant-overhead distillation
protocols based on codes with nonvanishing encoding rate
[11, 13–23]. Such approaches reduce the overhead per
output by processing many magic states within a growing
code block, making their performance at large numbers
of logical qubits particularly important to assess.
The need to evaluate these protocols has motivated re-
cent simulation algorithms and software libraries tailored
to non-Clifford quantum error correction [24–28]. These
methods substantially extend the range of accessible cir-
cuits, but generally retain potentially exponential runtime
dependencies. In particular, Surti, Daguerre, and Kim
[28] establish polynomial scaling in the number of physi-
cal qubits for broad families of magic state preparation
protocols when the numberk of logical qubits is fixed.
Their complexity also depends on a stabilizer or Pauli
decomposition of the target logical state, whose size can
arXiv:2610.06811v1  [quant-ph]  5 Oct 2026

2
grow exponentially withk. Consequently, efficient simula-
tion at fixedk does not guarantee efficient simulation of
high-rate protocols, where the number of logical outputs
grows with the block size.
This motivates simulation methods that scale polyno-
mially in both the physical and logical system sizes, even
as the number of non-Clifford operations grows. In this
work, we identify a broad class of circuits relevant to
fault-tolerant magic state preparation that admit such
simulation under stochastic Pauli noise.
A. Summary of results
We identify a class of non-Clifford quantum circuits
that admit classical simulation in time polynomial in the
number of qubits and the circuit size. The supported
operations are:
•Preparation of|0⟩and|+⟩states;
•Pauli,CNOT, andTgates;
• Arbitrary Z-type Pauli measurements and compati-
bleX-type Pauli measurements.
To describe the measurement restriction, we first iden-
tify the states generated by the unitary operations
above. Athird-order phase polynomial (3PP) stateis a
uniform-magnitude superposition over an affine subspace
of computational-basis labels (that is, the solution set of a
system of linear equations overF2), with phases specified
by a third-order phase polynomial overZ8. These are
exactly the states obtainable from the specified prepa-
rations using Pauli,CNOT, andT gates. A projective
Pauli measurement iscompatiblewith a 3PP state if the
post-measurement state is a 3PP state for every outcome
having nonzero probability. We call circuits composed of
the operations above, with each measurement compatible
on every reachable branch,3PP circuits.
The simulation maintains an exact, efficient represen-
tation of the conditional state throughout the circuit
and samples intermediate measurement outcomes ac-
cording to their probabilities. It also supports exact
evaluation of computational-basis amplitudes in a fixed
global-phase convention, exact Pauli expectation values,
computational-basis sampling, and equality testing be-
tween represented states up to global phase. Stochastic
Pauli noise is incorporated by sampling faults and evolv-
ing the corresponding conditional states, provided that
measurements remain compatible along the noisy trajec-
tories.
Alongside the phase-polynomial description, we develop
an equivalentdiagonal-Clifford-and-Pauli (DCP) stabi-
lizer formalism, in which states are uniquely specified,
up to global phase, by stabilizing operators generated by
Pauli gates and diagonal Clifford gates. We prove that a
state is a DCP stabilizer state if and only if it is a 3PP
state. We establish structural properties of DCP stabilizer
groups, construct efficient tableau representations, and
develop efficient update rules for both the tableau and
phase-polynomial descriptions.
We give necessary and sufficient conditions for anX-
type Pauli measurement to be compatible, together with
polynomial-time compatibility tests and conditional-state
updates. These results extend efficient simulation be-
yond unitary CNOT-dihedral circuits—circuits made of
T, CNOT, and Pauli gates [29]—to circuits with interme-
diate measurements and polynomial-time classical feed-
forward.
Our main result for quantum error correction reduces
compatibility under noise to a condition on the ideal
circuit. Theorem 7 shows that, if every idealX mea-
surement is deterministic or anticommutes with a current
Z-type stabilizer, then every measurement remains com-
patible under arbitrary inserted DCP faults, provided
that outcome-dependent operations are restricted to DCP
corrections. Thus, these ideal-circuit conditions certify
exact polynomial-time simulation of the entire noisy pro-
tocol, without a separate analysis of noisy branches. This
covers broad families of magic state preparation protocols,
including distillation, cultivation, and code switching, as
well as non-Clifford logical operations interleaved with
syndrome extraction.
Beyond circuits that preserve the DCP state class, we
introduceDCP rank: the minimum number of DCP sta-
bilizer states needed to express a state as a linear com-
bination. Such decompositions extend the framework to
universal circuit simulation, with a cost that depends on
the number of terms. Since every Pauli stabilizer state
is a DCP state, DCP rank is no greater than stabilizer
rank, although it can still grow exponentially.
Finally, we providemerlin [30], an open-source imple-
mentation of our simulation methods. We compare its
runtime and memory usage with existing non-Clifford
simulation libraries on distillation, cultivation, and code
switching circuits. The benchmarks demonstrate im-
proved scaling as the number of logical outputs grows in
Bravyi-Haah distillation [3], and simulation of a recent
code switching circuit based on a[[81,3,5]]code [16] on
which all other tested libraries exhaust memory or fail.
They also illustrate the complementary strengths of exist-
ing methods, which achieve lower per-shot runtimes on
the single-output distillation and cultivation examples.
B. Related work
1. Non-Clifford simulation algorithms
Classical simulation beyond Clifford circuits includes
stabilizer decompositions [31, 32], quasiprobability and
Pauli-propagation methods [33, 34], and hybrid stabilizer–
tensor-network representations [35, 36]. Recent work has
adapted and optimized these approaches for quantum
error correction. Tsim [24] combines stabilizer decompo-
sitions with ZX-calculus simplification and parametric
compilation [37, 38], while cultivation-specific decomposi-

3
tions demonstrate that circuit structure can substantially
reduce the number of required Clifford terms [39, 40].
SOFT [41], Clifft [25], and SymFT [27] combine stabilizer
or Clifford-frame descriptions with explicit coefficients for
non-stabilizer degrees of freedom. Other approaches in-
clude sparse Pauli-frame representations with truncation
[42] and polynomial-memory symbolic simulation through
auxiliary qubits, as implemented in SyQMA [26].
More specifically exploiting magic-state preparation,
Surti, Daguerre, andKim[28]propagatecircuit-levelPauli
faults into Clifford errors, with complexity depending on
a decomposition of the target magic state. Magdalena
de la Fuente et al. [43] develop a complementary space-
time approach for efficiently sampling syndrome statistics
in a class of non-Clifford CSS-type circuits. Our ap-
proach establishes polynomial-time simulation for circuits
preserving the DCP state class, including compatible in-
termediate measurements, without requiring a smallT
count or a small number of logical magic states.
2. SimulatingCNOT +Tcircuits
The unitary part of 3PP circuits consists ofCNOT, T,
and Pauli gates acting on qubits initialized in|0⟩or|+⟩.
Circuits with this gate set have been studied in several
contexts in the quantum information literature.
This gate set is closely related to the Z-network formu-
lation of Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial-time (IQP)
circuits [44, Section 5]. With|+⟩inputs andX-basis mea-
surements, Z-networks generated byCNOT, X, andeiθZ
are equivalent to IQP sampling up to efficient classical
post-processing. Restricting the rotation angles to integer
multiples ofπ/8gives our gate set, up to global phase. For
this choice of angles, Shepherd subsequently showed that
marginal distributions on logarithmically many output
bits can be computed efficiently [45, Theorem 4].
The unitary group generated byCNOT, T, and Pauli
gates was subsequently named the CNOT-dihedral group
and studied in the contexts of randomized benchmarking
and circuit synthesis [29, 46, 47]. Efficient polynomial
representations and composition algorithms were estab-
lished in [29], while [46] developed a finite presentation
using phase polynomials.
Here, we characterize whenX measurements preserve
the class of 3PP states and derive efficient conditional-
state update rules, enabling simulation of circuits with
intermediate measurements satisfying these conditions.
3. Generalized stabilizer formalisms
Our DCP stabilizer formalism can be seen as a special
case of themonomial stabilizer formalismintroduced by
Van den Nest [48], whose stabilizers are products of diag-
onal unitaries and permutation matrices. DCP operators
have this form because PauliX operators are permuta-
tion matrices. In this framework, each stabilizer state is
an equal-magnitude superposition of computational-basis
states, connected by the stabilizers’ permutation actions.
For DCP states, these actions are binary translations, so
the support is an affine subspace. Our phase-polynomial
representation satisfies the monomial framework’s suffi-
cient conditions for efficient computational-basis sampling
and approximate local expectation values. We addition-
ally provide exact Pauli expectation values and efficient
gate and compatible-measurement updates.
More directly, the XS formalism [49] uses tensor prod-
ucts ofX andS operators with eighth-root global phases.
XS states already admit affine-support, third-order phase-
polynomial descriptions, but with additional constraints
linking the polynomials to the support. By allowingCZ
factors in stabilizers, DCP removes these constraints and
captures all third-order phase-polynomial states.
The XP formalism [50] further generalizes XS to ar-
bitrary phase precision. Its measurement analysis [50,
Section 7 and Appendix F] provides outcome probabili-
ties for XP measurements and updates for diagonal Pauli
measurements. For PauliX measurements, its relative-
phase calculation corresponds to the discrete derivative of
the phase polynomial in our formulation. However, it does
not classify when non-diagonal measurements preserve
the XP state class. We provide such a classification for
DCP states, together with polynomial-time compatibility
tests and post-measurement updates.
Note added.While completing this manuscript, we
became aware of two independent related works on ef-

correction. Takada, Bartlett, and Williamson [51] de-

precise relationships and practical tradeoffs between these
approaches is an interesting direction for future work.

II. PHASE-POLYNOMIAL STATES AND
CIRCUITS
Our simulation framework rests on a simple description
of states prepared by Pauli,CNOT, andT gates: a uni-
form superposition over an affine space, with phases spec-
ified by a third-order polynomial. We first introduce the
CNOT-dihedral group and its phase-polynomial descrip-
tion, then define the corresponding class of third-order
phase-polynomial (3PP) states. Finally, we introduce
compatible measurements, which preserve this state class,
and use them to specify the circuits considered throughout
the paper.

IV. THE DCP STABILIZER FORMALISM
We now develop an equivalent stabilizer description of
3PP states, generalizing the Pauli stabilizer formalism
by allowing products of diagonal Clifford and Pauli op-
erators as stabilizers. They provide a Heisenberg-picture
representation suited to tracking non-Clifford circuits. We
establish the structure and canonical representation of
those stabilizer groups, then prove that the states they
uniquely specify are exactly the 3PP states.

VI. APPLICATIONS TO QUANTUM ERROR
CORRECTION
We now establish sufficient conditions for non-Clifford
quantum error-correction circuits to admit efficient simu-
lation under stochastic Pauli noise, and more generally
under stochastic DCP faults. The main challenge is to
ensure that everyX measurement remains compatible
with the 3PP representation, including after earlier faults
and measurements have changed the state.
We first identify two useful measurement mechanisms:
checks that are deterministic in the absence of noise, and
X measurements that anticommute with a currentZ-type
stabilizer. We prove that in the ideal case, the former
are compatible case (2a), and change to other cases with
noise, while the latter are compatible case (1). We then
prove that circuits whose idealX measurements are either
deterministic or case (1) remain compatible throughout
arbitrary sequences of DCP faults and measurements.
This reduces the analysis of noisy circuits to conditions on
theiridealevolution. Weapplytheseresultstomagicstate
distillation and transversal computation, DCP observable
checks used in magic state cultivation, gauge fixing and
code switching, teleportation, and cat-state syndrome
extraction.

C. Examples
We now apply the preceding results to several classes
of quantum error correction circuits.
1. Magic state distillation and non-Clifford computation
Logical CNOT-dihedral gates implemented by physical
CNOT-dihedral circuits, interleaved with CSS stabilizer
checks, directly satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 7: each
ideal gate preserves the code space, so the subsequent
checks are deterministic. This covers magic state distilla-
tion based on (generalized) triorthogonal codes, including
transversal and quasitransversal constructions with di-
agonal Clifford corrections [2–4, 18, 23, 55, 56]. It also
covers Litinski’s reformulation of these protocols as se-
quences ofZ-type π/8rotations followed byX readouts
[5], since these rotations are CNOT-dihedral and the
syndrome readouts are ideally deterministic. The same
argument applies to recent code constructions supporting
logical non-Clifford gates through constant-depth physical
circuits ofZ,CZ, andCCZ gates, including algebraic ge-
ometry constructions [13–15, 17, 20, 21, 23] and quantum
LDPC constructions based on cup products [43, 57–60].
These gates can be used either for magic state preparation
or for direct non-Clifford computation: in both settings,
subsequent CSS stabilizer checks are ideally deterministic,
so Theorem 7 applies.

3. Gauge fixing, code switching, and teleportation
Consider a CSS subsystem code whose stabilizer eigen-
values are fixed. Measuring a complete set of independent
Z-type gauge operators fixes the gauge qubits in aZ ba-

anticommutes with a currently fixedZ-type gauge opera-

compatibility under DCP faults.

IX. DISCUSSION
We have developed an exact polynomial-time simula-
tion framework for a broad class of non-Clifford circuits,
with no restriction on the number of logical qubits or
non-Clifford gates. It rests on two equivalent descrip-
tions: third-order phase-polynomial states, specified by
affine supports and polynomial phases, and DCP stabi-
lizer states, which extend the stabilizer picture beyond
Pauli operators and yield a tableau-based simulator. We
characterize precisely which Pauli measurements preserve
this state class and provide efficient tests and update
rules supporting intermediate measurements and classical
feedforward.
For quantum error correction, a simple condition on
the ideal circuit guarantees compatibility under arbitrary
inserted DCP faults: each idealX measurement must ei-
ther be deterministic or anticommute with a fixedZ-type
constraint. This remains sufficient even when noise makes
deterministic outcomes random, establishing efficient sim-
ulation for protocols involving magic state preparation,
non-Pauli checks, and CSS gauge fixing.
Our open-source implementation,merlin [30], makes
this framework available for circuit-level simulation.
Benchmarks show favorable runtime and memory scal-
ing with the number of logical outputs in Bravyi-Haah
distillation, and successful simulation of the distance-5
code switching circuit on which all other tested simula-
tors exhaust memory or fail. On the other hand, existing
methods currently achieve lower per-shot runtimes on the
single-output distillation and cultivation examples.
A natural next step is to use this framework to evalu-
ate recent proposals for non-Clifford computation with
quantum LDPC codes, including transversal gates, code
switching, and high-rate magic state preparation, when-
ever their circuit implementations satisfy our compati-
bility conditions. Combined with suitable decoders, our
simulator could enable direct comparisons of logical error
rates, acceptance probabilities, and resource overheads at
system sizes beyond the reach of existing methods.
An important direction is to extend the range of sup-
ported measurement gadgets. Some magic state distilla-
tion protocols based on gate injection, such as the parity-
checking constructions of Campbell and Howard [70], fall
outside the sufficient conditions of Theorem 7 and there-
fore require a separate compatibility analysis. Extending
our guarantees to cover these protocols would broaden
the scope of the simulator. Another possibility is to de-
scribe states in a rotated local Clifford basis, potentially
bringing Hadamard-based cultivation within the frame-
work [65, 71]. Although such a basis change can map the
Hadamard observable to a DCP operator, compatibility
must also be established for the transformed preparations,
gates, and measurements throughout the protocol.
Finally, the DCP decompositions introduced here sug-
gest a route to circuits that leave the 3PP state class:
understanding which fault-tolerant protocols admit small
DCP decompositions could extend the practical scope of
the simulator beyond the polynomial-time regime estab-
lished in this work.
