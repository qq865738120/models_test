The typical and averaged sizes of zeta sums have also been studied by Harper [ 8], while
Gao [3] obtained conditional upper bounds for their moments. These results concern
direct upper bounds, moments, or large-value constructions. The question considered
here is different: we ask what the existence of one abnormally large sum forces about
the zero set of ζ(s). Thus the main contribution is the pointwise inverse implication,
rather than the conditional upper bound by itself. Related analogues for sums of Fourier
coefficients of cusp forms were studied by Lamzouri [ 10], and more recently by by
Fr´ echette, Gerbelli-Gauthier, Hamieh and Tanabe [2].
The argument is modeled on the work of Granville and Soundararajan on large
character sums and zeros of Dirichlet L-functions [6]. Their method combines Hal´ asz’s
theorem and Lipschitz estimates for mean values of multiplicative functions with a
Gaussian transform and a zero-repulsion estimate obtained from the Hadamard product.
For the present function nit there are two changes that must be kept explicit. First, the
associated Dirichlet series is ζ(s− it), so all zero neighborhoods are translated to height
approximately t. Second, ζ has a pole at s = 1, and the corresponding residue appears
when the contour in the Gaussian transform is shifted.
