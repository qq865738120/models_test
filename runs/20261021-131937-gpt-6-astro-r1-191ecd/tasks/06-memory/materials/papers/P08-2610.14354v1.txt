No-Go Theorem for Phantom Crossing in
Scalar-Tensor Gravity with Non-Minimal Derivative
Coupling and Soft Scale-Invariance Breaking
Reza Baghbani∗1
1Department of Physics, Payame Noor University, Tehran, Iran
Abstract
Recent baryon acoustic oscillation measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic In-
strument (DESI 2024) reveal a preference for dynamical dark energy with an equation of
state parameter crossing the phantom divide ( w < −1) at low redshifts. We investigate
whether this phantom crossing can be realized within scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories
featuring non-minimal derivative coupling (NMDC) between the scalar field and the Einstein
tensor. Employing the effective field theory of dark energy formalism, we systematically
derive the ghost-free and gradient stability conditions, and examine their compatibility with
the swampland conjectures from quantum gravity. We establish three rigorous no-go theo-
rems demonstrating that: (i) phantom crossing necessitates ξeff< 0, directly violating the
no-ghost stability requirement ξeff> 0; (ii) the swampland de Sitter and distance conjectures
independently obstruct phantom crossing; and (iii) these theoretical obstructions are mutually
reinforcing. Our results show that scale-invariant NMDC models cannot account for the
DESI preference for w < −1, providing robust theoretical guidance for dark energy model
building beyond this class.
Keywords: Phantom crossing; Scale-invariant gravity; Non-minimal derivative coupling;
Swampland conjectures; DESI 2024

1. Introduction
The standard cosmological model, ΛCDM, has achieved remarkable success in describing a wide
range of cosmological observations, from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropies [1]
to the large-scale structure of the universe [2]. However, recent years have witnessed the
emergence of significant tensions between different observational datasets, most notably the
Hubble constant (H0) tension [3,4]. The local measurements of H0 from the SH0ES collaboration
yield H0 = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc [5], while the inference from Planck CMB data assuming
ΛCDM gives H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc [1], resulting in a discrepancy of approximately 5 σ. This
tension, if not attributable to systematic errors, may signal the need for new physics beyond the
standard model [6].
The situation has become even more intriguing with the release of the first-year data from the
Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) in 2024 [7,8]. The DESI baryon acoustic oscillation
(BAO) measurements, when combined with CMB data, show a preference for dynamical dark
energy with a time-varying equation of state w(z) that crosses the phantom divide ( w = −1) at
low redshifts [9,10]. This result, if confirmed by future data, would challenge the cosmological
constant paradigm and suggest that dark energy is a dynamical entity rather than a fixed property
of spacetime [11].
Scalar-tensor theories of gravity, which extend general relativity by introducing additional
scalar degrees of freedom coupled to the metric [12,13], provide a natural framework for modeling
dynamical dark energy. Among these, models with non-minimal derivative coupling (NMDC),
where the scalar field couples to the Einstein tensor via a termξGµν∂µϕ∂νϕ [14,15], have attracted
considerable attention due to their rich phenomenology and ability to modify the propagation of
gravitational waves [16,17]. Recent work by Ferrari et al. [18] has shown that certain scalar-tensor
models, such as the Brans-Dicke-Galileon (BDG) model, can reduce the Hubble tension to 1 .2σ
and naturally exhibit phantom-like behavior consistent with DESI’s preference for dynamical
dark energy.
However, the viability of these models must be assessed not only against observational data but
also against theoretical constraints from quantum gravity. The swampland program [19,20,22] has
proposed a set of conjectures that aim to distinguish effective field theories that can be consistently
coupled to quantum gravity (the “landscape”) from those that cannot (the “swampland”). These
conjectures, including the de Sitter conjecture, the distance conjecture, and the refined swampland
conjecture, impose stringent constraints on scalar field potentials and field excursions, with
profound implications for inflation and dark energy [23,24].
In this work, we investigate the possibility of phantom crossing ( wφ < −1) in scale-invariant
scalar-tensor theories with NMDC, subject to the combined constraints of ghost-free stability
and swampland conjectures. Scale invariance, which posits that the fundamental laws of physics
are invariant under global rescalings of the metric and fields, provides an elegant framework for
addressing the hierarchy problem and has been extensively studied in the context of inflation
and dark energy [25, 26]. The combination of scale invariance with NMDC coupling yields a
theoretically motivated model with rich cosmological dynamics [27].
Our main results are three no-go theorems that establish the impossibility of phantom crossing
in this class of models [28,29]. Theorem 1 demonstrates that phantom crossing is incompatible
with ghost-free stability in the slow-roll regime, as the former requires ξeff< 0 while the latter
requires ξeff> 0 [30]. Theorem 2 shows that even if the stability constraint could be circumvented,
the swampland conjectures impose an independent no-go condition, requiring c >

establish a robust no-go theorem that holds under the joint constraints of stability and swampland

These results have important implications for the interpretation of the DESI 2024 data [34].
While our no-go theorems do not rule out the possibility of phantom crossing in general, they
do show that scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories with NMDC cannot account for the DESI

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the scale-invariant
scalar-tensor model with NMDC and perform the conformal transformation to the Einstein frame.
In Section 3, we employ the effective field theory of dark energy formalism to extract the key

summarize our findings and discuss possible extensions.

5.5. Summary of Swampland Constraints
The swampland constraints provide three independent no-go results:
1. Stability constraint: Phantom crossing requires ξeff< 0, but the no-ghost condition
requires ξeff> 0 [28].
2. Distance constraint: The field excursion is limited to ∆ φ ≲ d · MP , which restricts the
kinetic energy available for phantom crossing [58].
3. de Sitter constraint: The potential must satisfy |U ′|/U ≥ c, which limits the flatness
required for slow-roll phantom behavior [22].
These constraints are mutually reinforcing and provide a robust no-go theorem for phantom
crossing in scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories with NMDC [24,67].

6.4. Implications for DESI 2024
The DESI 2024 BAO data show a preference for dynamical dark energy with w < −1 at low
redshifts [7–9]. Our no-go theorems imply that this preference cannot be explained within the
framework of scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories with NMDC.
This does not mean that the DESI results are incorrect, but rather that:
1. The true theory of dark energy must lie outside the class of models considered here.
2. Alternative explanations include: (i) breaking of scale invariance, (ii) additional fields or
interactions [35], (iii) modified gravity beyond scalar-tensor theories [36], or (iv) systematic
effects in the data analysis.
3. The no-go theorems provide a powerful tool for model selection: any model that claims to
explain the DESI results must explicitly address these constraints [68].
We will discuss possible extensions of our model in Section 8.

7.3. Comparison with Galileon Models
Galileon models [69,70] are a special class of scalar-tensor theories with second-order equations
of motion. The cubic Galileon term G3(ϕ, X)□ϕ (where X = − 1
2(∂ϕ)2) shares some similarities
with our NMDC coupling.
The key differences are:
1. Structure: Galileon models have G3 ∝ X, while our NMDC coupling involves the Einstein
tensor Gµν.
2. Equations of motion: Galileon models maintain second-order equations, while NMDC
models generally have higher-order equations (though they can be made ghost-free).
3. Phantom crossing: Some Galileon models can achieve phantom crossing without ghosts
[70], but this requires specific forms of G3 that are not compatible with scale invariance.
In the context of DESI 2024, Ref. [18] showed that the Brans-Dicke-Galileon (BDG) model
can reduce the Hubble tension to 1 .2σ and naturally exhibits phantom crossing. However, their
model breaks scale invariance explicitly.
Our no-go theorem applies specifically to scale-invariant theories with NMDC. If scale
invariance is broken (as in the BDG model), the no-go theorem may be evaded. This suggests
that scale invariance is a key ingredient in our no-go result.

Our analysis complements their work by providing a theoretical perspective. While Ferrari

Important distinction. The BDG model of Ferrari et al. breaks scale invariance explicitly
through the Galileon term. Our no-go theorem applies to scale-invariant theories, so it does not
directly constrain the BDG model. However, it does suggest that if one insists on maintaining
scale invariance (for theoretical reasons such as naturalness or UV completion), then phantom
crossing becomes problematic.

8.2. Implications for DESI 2024
Our no-go theorems have important implications for the interpretation of the DESI 2024 results:
1. Model selection: The preference for w < −1 in DESI data cannot be explained within the
framework of scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories with NMDC. This provides a powerful
tool for model selection: any model that claims to explain the DESI results must explicitly
address these constraints.
2. Theoretical guidance: Our results suggest that if the DESI preference for phantom
crossing is confirmed by future data, the true theory of dark energy must lie outside the
class of scale-invariant NMDC models. This could mean:
• Breaking of scale invariance (as in the BDG model of Ref. [18]),
• Additional fields or interactions beyond the minimal setup [35],
• Modified gravity beyond scalar-tensor theories [36],
• Or systematic effects in the data analysis that need to be carefully examined [72].
3. Complementarity: Our theoretical analysis complements the observational work of
Ferrari et al. [18]. While they focused on the observational fit of specific models, we have
provided a theoretical framework for understanding why certain classes of models face
fundamental obstacles.

8.3. Possible Extensions and Future Directions
Our no-go theorems apply specifically to scale-invariant theories with NMDC. Relaxing any of
the key assumptions may allow phantom crossing, but at the cost of losing some of the desirable
features of the model. Possible extensions include:
Breaking scale invariance: If scale invariance is explicitly broken (as in the BDG model),
the no-go theorem may be evaded. However, this introduces the question of naturalness: why
should the scale-breaking terms have the specific forms required to achieve phantom crossing?
Additional fields: Introducing additional scalar fields or coupling to other sectors (e.g.,
dark matter) may provide new degrees of freedom that allow phantom crossing while maintaining
stability. This is an interesting direction for future work.
Beyond Horndeski: Our model belongs to a subclass of Horndeski theory. More general
Horndeski theories, or even beyond-Horndeski theories (e.g., DHOST theories [36]), may have more
freedom to achieve phantom crossing. However, these theories often face their own theoretical
challenges.
Quantum corrections: Our analysis was performed at the classical level. Quantum
corrections to the effective action may modify the stability conditions and potentially allow
phantom crossing. This requires a detailed analysis of the quantum effective action, which is
beyond the scope of the present work.
Observational tests: Future data from DESI (full survey) [9], Euclid [73, 74], and other
cosmological surveys [75] will provide more precise constraints on the equation of state parameter
w(z). If the preference for w < −1 is confirmed with higher significance, it will provide strong
motivation for exploring models beyond the scale-invariant NMDC framework.
8.4. Final Remarks
In conclusion, we have established rigorous no-go theorems that forbid phantom crossing in
scale-invariant scalar-tensor theories with non-minimal derivative coupling, under the combined
constraints of ghost-free stability and swampland conjectures. These results provide important
theoretical guidance for model building in light of the DESI 2024 data and highlight the tension
between observational preferences and theoretical consistency.
Our work demonstrates the power of combining observational data with theoretical constraints
from quantum gravity (swampland conjectures [24,67]) to narrow down the space of viable dark
energy models [68]. As cosmological observations continue to improve in precision, such theoretical
frameworks will become increasingly important for interpreting the data and guiding the search
for the true nature of dark energy [76,77].
