UNIQUENESS AND STABILITY OF PLANAR SHOCK WAVE TO 2D
COMPRESSIBLE EULER EQUATIONS IN THE VANISHING VISCOSITY LIMIT OF
COMPRESSIBLE NAVIER-STOKES EQUATIONS
GENG CHEN, JEFFREY CHENG, ALEXIS F. VASSEUR, AND YI WANG
Abstract.It is well known that the compressible Euler equations with planar shock initial data may
admit infinitely many entropy weak solutions, as shown by Chiodaroli, De Lellis and Kreml [Comm. Pure
Appl. Math. (2015)]. In this paper, we introduce a suitable notion of global-in-time weak solutions to
the two-dimensional (2D) compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosities inR×T.
Within this solution framework, we establish the first vanishing viscosity limit selection criterion for the
physically relevant planar shock solution to the 2D compressible Euler equations. More precisely, we prove
the uniqueness and stability of the 2D Euler planar shock solution within a class of inviscid limits of Navier-
Stokes solutions emanating from near-planar initial data while the shock direction perturbation can be
arbitrarily large. Starting from the relativeκ-entropy for 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equations inT2 by
Bresch, Noble and Vila [ESAIM Proc. Surveys (2017)], we first establish the relative1
2-entropy between 2D
weak solution of compressible Navier-Stokes equations inR×Tand one-dimensional (1D) strong solution
for averaged initial data inR. Then we achieve the desired vanishing viscosity limit selection criterion
by successfully bridging the fundamental structural gap between 2D Eulerian formulation of the original
Navier-Stokes system in the relative1
2-entropy and 1D vanishing viscosity limit to a shock solution of Euler
equations in Lagrangian coordinates by Kang and Vasseur [Invent. Math. (2021)]. The framework developed
herein can be applied to the three-dimensional limit selection criterion inR×T2, as well as to more complex
planar Riemann solutions and interacting wave patterns.

For the compressible Navier-Stokes models with constant viscosities, that is, when both viscositiesµ(ρ)
andλ(ρ)are constants, the global existence of multi-dimensional weak solutions can be traced back to the
classical works by Lions [45], Jiang and Zhang [39], and Feireisl, Novotný and Petzeltová [32], while the
well-posedness of small smooth solutions near a constant equilibrium state was established by Matsumura
and Nishida [48]. However, from the Chapman–Enskog expansion of the Boltzmann equation, viscosity is
intrinsically a temperature-dependent transport coefficient, which in turn reduces to a density-dependent
function in the barotropic regime [15]. It turns out that the viscosity is expected to degenerate near vacuum
(ρ= 0) as a power of the density, which corresponds to the model we have taken above where the viscosity
degenerates linearly in the density near vacuum.
We are interested in the limitν→0+for the selection criteria of 2D physical planar shock. Formally, we
recover the 2D compressible Euler equations:(

There is a large body of work on the idea of using the vanishing viscosity limit to establish existence
and stability results for inviscid gases. In the 1D setting, the vanishing viscosity limit using the so-called
“artificial viscosity” (when the viscosity does not depend on the density, and there is also an artificial diffusion
on the continuity equation) has been used to construct entropy solutions to the isentropic Euler system (see
[28], [35]). The same limit was shown to be valid and stable in the case when the limit solution is small-BV
[3]. The vanishing physical viscosity limit to the small-BV solution, i.e. the limit from Navier-Stokes to
Euler equations, is a much harder problem, which was solved recently for the isentropic case in one space
dimension, see [40], [19]. Also see the recent work [1] for a detailed description of shock formation in the
vanishing viscosity limit, and the earlier result for the limit in the relative energy space [22] using the method
of compensated compactness.
In the multi-dimensional setting, the vanishing viscosity limit has been established for short times [16],
[33], [4]. The short-time limitation corresponds to the fact that the limiting Euler solution must be a strong
solution, which exists only locally in time. There are very few results that hold after the time of shock
formation; let us mention the works of Zumbrun et. al ([36], [37], [38]) which construct smooth viscous
shocks converging to curved inviscid shocks as the viscosity tends to zero.
However, to the best of our knowledge, no existing results have established the vanishing physical viscosity
as a uniqueness criterion for multi-dimensional planar shock waves, while simultaneously verifying both the
validity and stability of the multi-dimensional vanishing viscosity limit in the regime where the solutions to
system (1.1) are themselves weak and potentially discontinuous. The aim of the present paper is to provide
some results in this direction, in the case when the limiting inviscid profile is a single planar shock. It is
important to study the inviscid limit to the compressible Euler equations because it is a natural option to
select physical solution for Euler equations in multiple space dimensions. It is well known that the entropy
BV solution of Euler equations in one space dimension is unique [11, 12, 17, 18, 20, 21]. However, the entropy
condition is not strong enough to select a unique solution for Euler equations in multiple spatial dimensions,
when there is an initial shock wave, even just a planar shock. In a seminal paper [24], Chiodaroli, De Lellis
and Kreml constructed infinitely many 2D solutions starting from the same planar initial shock, using the
method of convex integration (see also [27]). We refer readers to [47], [46], [42], for subsequent non-uniqueness
results associated to more general Riemann data. See also results showing density of the set of wild initial
