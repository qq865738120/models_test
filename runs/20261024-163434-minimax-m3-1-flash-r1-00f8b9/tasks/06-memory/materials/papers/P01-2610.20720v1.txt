Nonlocal modifications of gravity provide a theoretically motivated framework in which cosmic
acceleration can emerge dynamically without introducing a fundamental cosmological constant.
We revisit the cosmological predictions of the Maggiore–Mancarella nonlocalR□−2Rmodel, with
particular emphasis on its background dynamics and observational viability in light of recent DESI
baryon acoustic oscillation measurements. A key feature of the theory is its predictive character: for
fixed standard cosmological parameters, each value of the nonlocal parameter determines a specific
effective pair(w 0, wa), so that the model traces a restricted trajectory in the Chevallier–Polarski–
Linder plane rather than spanning the full phenomenological parameter space. We construct this
mapping explicitly and confront the model with observations by implementing its cosmological
dynamics in a Boltzmann solver and performing a Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis using Cosmic
Microwave Background, supernova, and DESI BAO data. We find that current datasets constrain
the nonlocal parameter close to theΛCDM limit, with no statistically significant preference for
nonlocal deviations.
I. INTRODUCTION
General Relativity (GR) provides an extremely successful description of gravitational phenomena over a vast range
of scales, from laboratory and Solar System tests to the large-scale dynamics of the Universe. Combined with the
cosmological principle and a cosmological constant, it leads to the standardΛCDM model, which provides an excellent
fit to a broad set of cosmological observations, including the cosmic microwave background (CMB) [1], large-scale
structure, and supernova distance measurements [2]. Despite this empirical success, the physical origin and the
observed small value of the cosmological constant remain theoretically unexplained, giving rise to a severe fine-tuning
problem. This has long motivated the exploration of theoretically consistent alternatives in which cosmic acceleration
emerges dynamically rather than from a fundamental cosmological constant [3, 4].
RecentobservationaldevelopmentsfurtherreinforcetheimportanceoftestingdeparturesfromΛCDM.Inparticular,
the latest baryon acoustic oscillation measurements from the DESI collaboration have reached unprecedented precision
andshowamildpreferencefordynamicaldark-energyparametrizationswhencombinedwithothercosmologicalprobes
[5]. While the statistical significance of these deviations is still under active assessment, see e.g. [6, 7], they provide
a timely opportunity to confront theoretically well-defined models of dynamical dark energy with high-quality data
[8–14]. In this context, predictive models, in which the dark-energy evolution is not freely adjustable but instead
follows from the underlying theory, are especially valuable.
An important distinction must therefore be made between phenomenological evidence for an evolving dark-energy
component and evidence for a specific theory of cosmic acceleration. While the CPL parametrization allows the
present value and time dependence of the dark-energy equation of state to vary independently, a fundamental theory
generally correlates these quantities through its equations of motion. Consequently, even a model capable of generating
a time-dependent equation of state need not reproduce the particular evolutionary pattern favored by observations.
Assessing this compatibility requires confronting the correlations imposed by the underlying dynamics with the data,
rather than merely establishing the existence of an evolving dark-energy component.
Among the proposed extensions of GR, nonlocal gravity constitutes a well-motivated class of modified theories
[15]. Nonlocal interactions naturally arise in several approaches to quantum gravity. In string theory, for instance,

gravitational interactions are expected to acquire a nonlocal character at the Planck scale [16, 17]. From an ultraviolet
perspective, nonlocality has also been investigated as a possible ingredient for improving the high-energy behavior
of gravity and constructing ultraviolet-complete theories [18–20]. Nonlocal modifications, however, are not restricted
to the ultraviolet regime. They can also emerge at large distances from several infrared mechanisms, including
quantum corrections to the effective gravitational action [21–24], effective field theories obtained after integrating
out light degrees of freedom [25, 26], more fundamental formulations of Euclidean quantum gravity [27, 28], effective
descriptions of massive-graviton theories [29, 30], theories involving multiple metrics [31], and extensions based on
post-Riemannian affine geometry [32]. The phenomenological implications of nonlocal gravity have also been explored
in astrophysical settings, including compact stellar objects [33].
At the level of the gravitational action, nonlocality is typically introduced through curvature invariants involving
either infinitely many derivatives, encoded in non-polynomial functions of the d’Alembertian operator [19], or inverse
differential operators such as□ −1 [23] or the inverse conformal anomaly operator [34]. The latter approach was
pioneered in the context of cosmology by Deser and Woodard (DW) [23], who proposed one of the first nonlocal
models aimed at explaining the late-time cosmic acceleration without explicitly introducing a cosmological constant.
The DW model modifies the evolution of cosmological perturbations in a way that was initially considered to be
in tension with observations [35]. Subsequent studies, however, showed that this tension could be alleviated [36],
although a comprehensive confrontation of the model with observational data remains outstanding.
In this work, we focus on the nonlocalR□−2Rmodel introduced by Maggiore and Mancarella (MM) [37]. In this
framework, deviations from GR are controlled by a single mass scale and manifest themselves at cosmological scales
as an effective dark-energy component. The model modifies the large-scale gravitational dynamics while preserving
the successful local predictions of GR and yielding a matter power spectrum compatible with current observations
[38–40]. More generally, the MM model and related realizations of nonlocal gravity have been shown to admit vi-
able cosmological backgrounds and consistent perturbation dynamics without the need for an explicit cosmological
constant [41, 42]. An important feature of the MM model is its predictive character: once the realization of the
inverse d’Alembertian operator is fixed through the initial conditions of the corresponding auxiliary fields, the cos-
mological evolution is no longer freely adjustable but is restricted to a narrow family of trajectories controlled by a
nonlocal mass parameter [43]. Consequently, the theory does not span the full space of phenomenological dark-energy
parametrizations; instead, its underlying dynamics impose a definite relation between the present-day value of the
effective equation of state and its time evolution.
The goal of this paper is to test these theoretically restricted dynamics against current cosmological data, with
particular emphasis on the recent DESI results [5]. We first review the background evolution of the model using a
dynamical-systems approach and identify the class of solutions capable of producing a slowly varying dark-energy
component around the present epoch. We then construct a local mapping between these solutions and the effective
CPL parameters(w 0, wa)[44, 45]. This mapping allows us to formulate the observational test in a particularly
transparent way. Rather than asking whether the model can reproduce isolated CPL histories, we determine the one-
dimensional trajectory that it predicts in the(w0, wa)plane and examine whether this trajectory intersects the region
favored by current data once the standard cosmological parameters are varied simultaneously. We perform this test by
implementing the cosmological dynamics in a Boltzmann solver and carrying out a Markov chain Monte Carlo analysis
using CMB, supernova, and DESI BAO measurements. We find that varying the standard cosmological parameters
does not substantially alter the restricted trajectory predicted by the model, and that current data constrain the
nonlocal dynamics close to itsΛCDM-like limit.
Beyond the numerical likelihood analysis, we show that the so-called path-B dynamics admit a simple local charac-
terization in the CPL plane. Close to theΛCDM-like endpoint, the effective parameters satisfy a consistency relation
of the formwa =−α NL(1 +w 0) +O((1 +w 0)2), where the coefficientαNL is determined by the underlying nonlocal
dynamics. This relation provides a geometrical interpretation of the observational constraints, but does not by itself
establish the viability of the model, since variations of the standard cosmological parameters can, in principle, dis-
place and broaden the theoretical trajectory. Determining whether this freedom is sufficient requires the full likelihood
analysis performed below.

their effective CPL interpretation. In Sec. IV we present the observational analysis and parameter constraints. We

V. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
In this work, we have investigated the cosmological dynamics and observational viability of the nonlocalR□−2R
model, focusing on the class of solutions known as path B. These solutions reproduce the standard radiation- and
matter-dominated epochs before approaching a quasi-de Sitter saddle point, where cosmic acceleration can persist for
an extended period. Unlike the asymptotic de Sitter-like behavior of path A, the accelerated stage along path B is
transient, eventually giving way to a radiation-like regime without radiation. Around the present epoch, however,
path B can generate expansion histories resembling those of dynamical dark-energy models.
To characterize these histories, we have constructed a local mapping between the nonlocal background evolution
and the effective CPL parameters(w0, wa). For fixed standard cosmological parameters, the dynamics restrict these
quantities to a one-dimensional trajectory rather than allowing them to vary independently. Close to theΛCDM-like
limit, we have shown that this trajectory obeys the consistency relationwa =−α NL(1 +w 0) +O((1 +w 0)2). By
expanding the nonlocal background equations in powers of√γ, we have determined its asymptotic slope, obtaining
αNL ≃0.686for our fiducial cosmological parameters. This result provides a quantitative connection between the
underlying gravitational dynamics and the direction of departure fromΛCDM in the CPL plane.
Although the path-B trajectory enters the quadrantw0 >−1,w a <0, also favored by phenomenological CPL
fits to DESI, its orientation differs from that preferred by the data. In particular, increasingly negative values of
wa necessarily correspond to values ofw0 farther above−1, preventing the model from exploring the full range of
independently varying CPL parameters. Our likelihood analysis, combining CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, and Pantheon+
observations, confirms that this restricted evolution does not provide sufficient freedom to reproduce the expansion
histories preferred by an unrestricted CPL fit. Instead, the posterior favors small values ofγ, driving the allowed
solutions toward theirΛCDM-like limit.
The comparison withΛCDM for a combined dataset of Planck+DESI DR2+Pantheon+ yields a modest improve-
ment in the minimum effective chi-squared,∆χ2

Our analysis is also restricted to the path-B realization defined by the auxiliary-field initial conditions adopted
