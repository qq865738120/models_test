Abstract.A cosmological constant Λ in a universe weakly violating the cosmological prin-
ciple (CP) can mimic a dynamical dark energy (DDE) in a FRW universe, and therefore
challenging the recently reported DDE evidences. We test this scenario in the form of ΛLTB
cosmology, against two data combinations. (1) DESI DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, together with
any of the four SNe Ia compilations (UNION3, Pantheon+, DES SN5YR, and DES Dovekie)
show no preference over DDE or ΛLTB. (2) The inclusion of the kinematic Sunyaev Zel’dovich
(kSZ) effect rules out a large portion of the CP violation parameter space allowed by (1).
However, the existence of a∼300 Mpc/hregion with∼10% matter density excess is still
allowed by the current data, and therefore remains a viable alternative to DDE. To facilitate
further tests, we present analytical expressions of the distance-redshift relation and the kSZ
effect in a universe with weak CP violation.

1 Introduction
The ΛCDM cosmology, being the standard model of modern cosmology, is challenged by
various cosmological observations. In particular, DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO)
measurements together with CMB, SNe Ia and weak gravitational lensing reveal deviations
from the ΛCDM model, pointing toward dynamical dark energy (DDE) exhibiting Quintom
behavior [1–5] . If verified, this will be a groundbreaking discovery of fundamental physics.
However, the DDE interpretation is obtained within the FRW framework, which assumes
the cosmological principle (CP) that the universe at sufficiently large scale is homogeneous
and isotropic [6]. CMB observations verify the isotropy of the universe viewed from us [7].
However, radial inhomogeneities are more difficult to rule out, since they are entangled with
temporal evolution along our past light cone [8, 9]. Such large-scale radial inhomogeneities
alter the distance-redshift relation and can in principle mimic the effects of DDE. Therefore,
a crucial task is to distinguish the two scenarios, namely a DDE in a FRW universe versus a
Λ in a non-FRW universe.
While DDE in a FRW universe has been extensively studied, the alternative possibility
has received comparatively less attention in this context. To examine such a non-FRW sce-
nario, we consider the Lemaˆ ıtre–Tolman–Bondi (LTB) spacetime [10–12] with a cosmological
– 1 –

constant, a spherically symmetric but radially inhomogeneous solution. For an observer lo-
cated near the symmetry center, the sky can remain nearly isotropic, consistent with cosmic
microwave background (CMB) observations, while light-cone observables may still differ from
their counterparts in the ΛCDM model.
Early LTB studies explored a more radical possibility: radial inhomogeneities could
entirely account for the accelerated cosmic expansion without a cosmological constant [13, 14].
This scenario is now severely constrained because large radial inhomogeneities can induce
large coherent bulk flows, and generate large kinetic Sunyaev–Zel’dovich (kSZ) signals [15–
19]. A milder approach retains the cosmological constant while introducing weak radial
inhomogeneities, leading to the ΛLTB model. Such models have been employed to describe
low-redshift distance observables [20–24]. Their consistency with kSZ constraints has also
been investigated, and specific radial profiles remain viable [23, 24]. Recent evidence for
DDE has renewed interest in whether deviations from ΛCDM could instead arise from cosmic
inhomogeneities [25] or local effects [26]. In this context, Camarena et al. recently confronted
a ΛLTB model with DESI-era cosmological observations [27].
However, efficient calculations in ΛLTB models remain challenging. Although exact
implicit solutions exist for the time evolution, evaluating the metric functions requires the
numerical inversion of elliptic integrals at every spacetime point. Furthermore, predicting
kSZ signals entails computationally demanding geodesic ray-tracing from off-center scattering
locations. These requirements substantially hinder efficient exploration of the cosmological
parameter space. Consequently, a fast, analytical framework capable of computing both
observables is strongly needed.
In this work, we develop such a framework. Since the departures from ΛCDM reported
by DESI are mild, implying a universe with weak CP violation if attributed to radial inhomo-
geneities, we thus treat the LTB metric as a first-order perturbation around a spatially flat
FRW background. Substituting this perturbed metric into the Einstein field equations, we
solve the linearized equations perturbatively to obtain explicit expressions for the metric func-
tions. By solving the null geodesic equations along our past light cone, we express the space-
time coordinates as functions of redshift. Combining these light-cone trajectories with the
metric-defined transverse and radial distances, we derive explicit analytic distance–redshift
relations. To incorporate the kSZ information, we then model the kSZ angular power spec-
trum. Because kSZ predictions are most naturally formulated in the conformal Newtonian
gauge, we map the perturbed LTB solution into this gauge via a first-order coordinate trans-
formation, exploiting the fact that both describe linear perturbations around a flat FRW
background. This allows us to bypass computationally expensive geodesic ray-tracing and
directly derive the kSZ angular power spectrum using the standard remote-dipole formalism
[28, 29]. The resulting framework enables efficient joint likelihood analyses combining SNe
Ia compilations, BAO, and CMB data, both with and without kSZ constraints.
Applying this framework to four SNe Ia datasets (each combined with BAO and CMB
measurements), we find that the ΛLTB model yields fits broadly comparable to those of the
DDE model, both before and after imposing the kSZ constraint. Although the kSZ constraint
substantially restricts large radial inhomogeneities, a parameter region with a matter excess of
∼10% and a characteristic scale of∼300h −1Mpc remains viable. Thus, current observations
do not uniquely require a dynamical dark energy interpretation of distance-redshift relation,
leaving large-scale inhomogeneity as a viable alternative.

3 Constraining Radial Inhomogeneity with a kSZ Upper-bound Constraint
3.1 LTB-induced kSZ signal
The radial inhomogeneity not only alters light-cone distances but also drives a coherent
radial velocity within the CMB rest frame. A larger profile amplitude increases this velocity,
whereas a larger spatial scale maintains velocity coherence over larger radii. Consequently,
the kSZ angular power spectrum is sensitive to bothm 0,amp andr 0. To incorporate kSZ
constraints, we transform the first-order ΛLTB solution into the conformal Newtonian gauge,
in which standard kSZ calculations are naturally formulated [38]. We then compute the
resulting kSZ angular power spectrum.

4 Discussion and Conclusion
In this work, we develop a first-order perturbative framework for the ΛLTB model. We
then apply this framework to test whether large-scale radial inhomogeneity can serve as an
alternative to dynamical dark energy. We find that the ΛLTB model fits current distance
data comparably well tow 0waCDM, while kSZ constraints substantially restrict, but do not
rule out, the viable parameter space.
The preferred profile is qualitatively consistent with the DESI DR2 DDE result [2],
wherew 0 >−1 modifies the low-redshift distance–redshift relation in a manner analogous
to a local overdensity. Because our compensated profile transitions from an overdensity to
an underdensity with increasing radius, it can further mimic an apparent Quintom behav-
ior [5, 20, 43–45]. The kSZ constraint excludes profiles with both large amplitudes and
large characteristic scales, substantially reducing the allowed departure from the Coperni-
can principle. However, because the surviving low-kSZ profiles retain nearly the same peak
likelihood, the minimumχ 2 and AIC remain largely unchanged. Instead, the kSZ bound

Several recent ΛLTB analyses have examined whether a radial inhomogeneity can alle-
viate the Hubble tension (for a review of the Hubble tension, see [46]). Most of these studies
[23, 24, 47–49] favor an underdense profile, in contrast to our findings. This discrepancy is
largely driven by their inclusion of a SH0ES-like localH 0 prior, whereas our analysis does not
impose this prior. Furthermore, large-scale underdensities are generally insufficient to resolve
the Hubble tension [50]. Without assuming anH 0 prior, a recent analysis combining DESI
DR2 BAO, Planck CMB, and Pantheon+/DES Y5 SNe Ia data also favors an overdense pro-
file [27]. Both that study and the present work test whether large-scale radial inhomogeneities
can account for distance–redshift signatures typically attributed to dynamical dark energy. In
– 17 –

[PDF page 19]
contrast, Futamase et al. conducted a distinct yet complementary analysis [51]. Specifically,
they interpreted the localH 0 tension and the DESI DR2 BAO deviations from the standard
cosmology as possible signatures of cosmic inhomogeneities, using the combined dataset to
reconstruct a redshift-dependent matter density profile. In addition to matter density inho-
mogeneities, radial variations in dark-energy density arising from late-time first-order phase
transitions have also been explored [52, 53]. Recently, Friedman-Shaw et al. [53] showed that
the resulting dark-energy bubbles can produce distance–redshift signatures similar to DESI
BAO data. However, the parameter region capable of producing such signatures is ruled out
by CMB constraints, particularly those from kSZ measurements.
Beyond these observational considerations, our perturbative analytic framework offers
a computationally efficient alternative for solving the LTB equations, avoiding the use of
implicit elliptic integrals. While symbolic regression has recently been used to construct
closed-form LTB approximations [54], our derivation keeps the underlying physical connec-
tions among the metric, gravitational potentials, and velocity field fully explicit. Formulating
the solution in conformal Newtonian gauge also ensures compatibility with the standard cos-
mological perturbation framework. This enables straightforward extensions to observables
sensitive to velocity and gravitational potentials, such as redshift-space distortions, Doppler
magnification, and gravitational redshift. Furthermore, this gauge formulation substantially
streamlines the kSZ calculation. For off-center sources in LTB spacetimes, traditional kSZ
calculations integrate inward and outward null geodesics from each scattering location to
the last scattering surface. The resulting boundary redshifts (z ∗
+ andz ∗
−) are then used to
determine the effective velocity [15, 16, 18]. In contrast, our method evaluates the effective
velocity directly from the potentials and velocity field in the conformal Newtonian gauge.
This bypasses the need for repeated numerical integrations of null geodesics and yields an
explicit decomposition into Doppler, Sachs–Wolfe, and integrated Sachs–Wolfe contributions.
The present analysis has several limitations. First, exact spherical symmetry is an
idealization of large-scale inhomogeneities, and the adopted density profile represents a re-
stricted functional family. Second, while the first-order ΛLTB framework is sufficiently ac-
curate within the parameter space allowed by the current data, it breaks down when the
metric departs significantly from a FRW spacetime. Third, we approximate the electron
power spectrum in the LTB spacetime using the matter power spectrum of a matched FRW
background. This simplification neglects spacetime-inhomogeneity effects on clustering and
electron bias, as well as small-scale nonlinear physics. Future work should refine the kSZ
modeling, incorporate more general or physically motivated profiles, and relax the spherical
symmetry assumption to accommodate three-dimensional large-scale inhomogeneities.
Overall, our results demonstrate that large-scale inhomogeneity remains a viable alter-
native to dynamical dark energy for interpreting distance–redshift signatures in current data.
Although the kSZ constraint substantially restricts allowed departures from homogeneity, it
does not change the above conclusion. Meanwhile, the inferred deviation from the Coperni-
can principle remains dataset-dependent. To further distinguish these two interpretations,
future analyses could exploit the complementary information on cosmic expansion and struc-
ture growth provided by large-scale structure probes (for a review, see [55]). The first-order
ΛLTB framework developed here provides a unified, computationally efficient foundation
for future modeling, facilitating tests of large-scale inhomogeneity against complementary
cosmological probes.

D Accuracy of the first-order velocity expression
Eq. (3.11) provides a first-order expression for the radial peculiar velocity in the conformal
Newtonian gauge. Because both the LTB metric and the coordinate transformation are
truncated at linear order, it’s hard to retain a complete set of second-order terms. To assess
accuracy, we restore the terms omitted when reducingd˜r/d˜ηto∂ ηLin Eq. (3.11). These
include the Jacobian factord˜η/dηand the pullback between (˜η,˜r) and (η, r).
Fig. 10 demonstrates that these additional terms yield only a minor change in velocity,
even for an extreme stress-test profile (m 0,amp = 0.5,r 0 = 1200 Mpc). Their impact is even
smaller within the posterior region used in the main analysis, confirming the accuracy of
Eq. (3.11) at the required order.
